Internal Medicine

Benzodiazepines and z-hypnotics (non-benzodiazepine sleeping pills) are both prescription medications used to treat sleep problems, but they differ in important ways.

Key differences at a glance:

Benzodiazepines (e.g., temazepam, lorazepam) broadly activate GABA receptors in the brain, producing sedation, muscle relaxation, and anxiety relief. They carry a higher risk of dependence, tolerance, and next-day drowsiness.

Z-hypnotics (e.g., zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone) target GABA receptors more selectively, focusing on sleep. They tend to have fewer daytime side effects but can still cause dependence, memory issues, and complex sleep behaviors.

Safety considerations: Both drug classes may impair driving, increase fall risk in older adults, and interact dangerously with alcohol or opioids. Long-term use of either is generally discouraged.

Explanation

Benzodiazepines and sleeping pills, such as z-hypnotics, are both used to help people sleep, but they have some differences. Benzodiazepines affect a wide range of brain functions and are used for anxiety, seizures, and sleep. They can cause more side effects, like feeling sleepy during the day and possibly becoming dependent on them.

Z-hypnotics, which include drugs like zopiclone, zolpidem, and zaleplon, are a newer type of sleeping pill. They are designed to help with sleep problems specifically and may have fewer side effects than benzodiazepines. According to the references, z-hypnotics are considered safer and better for sleep because they focus more on helping with sleep and less on other brain functions.

In summary, while both benzodiazepines and z-hypnotics can help you sleep, z-hypnotics might be a better choice if you're looking for fewer side effects and a lower risk of dependency.
