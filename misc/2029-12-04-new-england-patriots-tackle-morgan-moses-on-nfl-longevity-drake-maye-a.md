# New England Patriots Tackle Morgan Moses on NFL Longevity, Drake Maye, and Super Bowl LX

WHETHER YOU NEED an offensive lineman to start every game for seven consecutive seasons or a teammate to break down his hyperbaric chamber and bring it to the team practice facility, Morgan Moses is your man. This season, Drake Maye and the Patriots learned just how dependable the veteran right tackle can be.

New England signed Moses last March as an unrestricted free agent. He was originally drafted in 2014 by Washington in the third round, and the 6’6,” 320-pounder has played in 183 career games for four teams (Washington, the New York Jets, Baltimore Ravens, and now the Patriots). From 2015 to 2020, Moses played all 96 games for Washington, logging over 1,000 snaps in three separate seasons.

That's not to say that Moses has always been healthy throughout his 12-year career. His numerous injuries have included a torn pectoral muscle, a knee fracture, and an MCL sprain and bone bruise last season. But his reliability and consistency on the field have helped earn his staying power—and his performance backs it up. During the playoff run that saw the Patriots facing strong defensive opponents (the Los Angeles Chargers, Houston Texans, and Denver Broncos), Moses was rock solid. Over the first two games, the right tackle didn't allow a single QB pressure and was named the Offensive Player of the Week by Pro Football Focus.

At 34, Moses is the oldest player on the Patriots roster. The respected veteran leader will look to help lead his team to victory at Super Bowl LX in Santa Clara on Sunday. But first, he spoke with Men’s Health from California about his connection to Maye and the Patriots other young players, his longevity “menu,” and his mindset before his first ever Super Bowl.

MEN'S HEALTH: You’ve played in the NFL for 12 years, and this is your first Super Bowl. How are you feeling?

MORGAN MOSES: I feel fantastic. It’s a long season, especially being 34 and year 12 in the NFL, playing O-line and all the good things. I was blessed to have some good veteran guys early in my career who took me under their wing and showed me the ropes of taking care of your body. As they say, ‘you can’t make the team in the tubs’—it’s 100 percent true. My first seven years in the league, I started 143 consecutive games, and I had a lot of surgeries in between those seasons. I pride myself on being available and taking care of my body and being able to recover.

MH: Over a decade into NFL playing life, what does recovery look like for you? What have you learned along the way?

MM: I’d be lying if I said I do something every day. Some days, you just need some TLC days. Earlier in my career, I was trying to embed hot yoga, eating right, making sure I was getting a certain amount of sleep, studying the playbook. Now, it’s kind of different. I had this conversation with one of my trainers who I wound up playing with in Washington for six years. We have these, 'Come to Jesus and Moses’ talks. This might’ve been four years ago now. I said, "Dude, when did you stop going so hard in the weight room?" He said, "Morgan, if I would’ve known what I know now and stopped going in the weight room and competing with these 23 year olds throwing 425 pounds on the bench, I probably would’ve played four more years." I said, "Okay, cool, because it hurts lifting that much now."

You do all these things earlier in your career to create the patent for yourself of what your body needs—whether it’s hyperbaric chamber, infrared saunas, cups, acupuncture—you try all these things to make a menu for yourself for throughout the week during the season. In the latter parts of your career, it’s like, less is more.

MH: Is that something you teach younger players like Will Campbell? [Campbell, the Patriots rookie left tackle, suffered a Grade 3 MCL sprain in his right knee during Week 12 against the Cincinnati Bengals. He returned to action in the playoffs].

MM: One hundred percent, though it’s one of those things where you don't want to throw too much on their plates. Will had never had an injury in his career—he didn’t know anything about red light therapy, etc. The dude thought his leg was going to get amputated.

So I’m trying to educate them to the point where they understand it. They already trust you. They see this dude who has played 1,000 years in the league … they see the make up of what you have done in your career. The one thing I tell them is that what is for me, may not be for you. You have to find out what is good and not good for you to be able to do those things. For me, that’s acupuncture. I can get it for two hours straight. I’ll do everything on my schedule—infrared sauna, hyperbaric chamber, cupping, IVs—and if I’m still missing something, it’s, "let me go get acupuncture." You have to make a recipe for yourself. Different weeks call for different things. Some weeks, you’re beat up more than others. You make a cocktail of things that you have that you know your body can respond to.

MH: I read that after Will’s injury, you literally packed up and brought your personal hyperbaric chamber to the Patriots facility.

MM: Yep, I packed up my hyperbaric chamber, pulled it down the stairs myself. I brought it to the facility–and a lot of guys ended up using it. It’s been there since November.

MH: You have a special connection with Drake Maye as well, as do your three sons. How would you describe him?

MM: With Drake, a lot of things that people don’t get to see is that who he is as a person to the media, to the nutritionist, to the Strength Coach, is what you see from him every day throughout the team. He’s a humble guy.

He has qualities of leadership you can’t even put your thumb on. Just the man he’s growing to be. I’m blessed to be able to play for a guy like that—and not even just play with, play for a guy like that, but to get to know him on another level. Being able to speak to his mom and dad and his brothers... I’m getting to know them a bit, [and it's] one of those things that the apple doesn’t fall far from the apple tree to see where his greatness lies. Who he is as a young quarterback, and the things that he’s done for this team and specifically for our offensive line room, picking us up when we’re down, and then just being one of the guys. He’s undeniable as our quarterback, as our leader, as our brother, you name it.

MH: What about the mental side of the game, especially this far into the season? Do you give the young players advice on how to stay steady?

MM: It’s a different game for those guys, especially the rookies coming in. Let’s say Will. He goes to LSU and plays with a national championship team. Then he goes right to the Senior Bowl, then the Combine, then he goes to his team workouts, then he has 30 visits with teams that are likely to draft him. Then he’s getting drafted, then it’s OTAs, then you’re in this locker room and holy shit, these are grown men!

There are so many things that go into it: learning a new scheme, not trying to be that guy that holds everyone back, and you're learning your way. In college, you might play two guys that are NFL ready. Then you go to the NFL and every week, the back up to the back up is that guy!

We started the season facing Maxx Crosby, then TJ Watt, then Myles Garrett. Then you’ve got the Texans, Chargers, Broncos... there’s no break where you can be like, "I know you’ll dominate this guy with pure athleticism." For me, being 12 years in and playing so much football, I tell everybody in the locker room—they laugh about this, but I’ll name guys I’ve blocked and they’re like, "Who’s Julius Peppers"’ and I’m like, "Bro, am I that old?"

[Morgan Moses](https://7161777.com/gn8k9qar1x/)

For me, I’ve been in the bright spot of the evolution of football. I came into the league blocking guys like Peppers, Mario Williams, these bigger D-ends. Now you have these Vonn Milers that are 240, 250, the more athletic pass rushers than the 6’5” 275-pound guy. So I tease everyone that my draft class was the last year of real football. I’ve gotten to see the evolution of what the defensive schemes have gone to. That being said, you understand that everyone runs the same thing, it’s just different personnel—whether a Watt or a Garrett, that’s the difference. Because everyone says this: Coaches coach, but players make it come to life. So different players make things look different than what you saw last week.

MH: You’ve played for several teams over the years, so you’ve seen a lot of personalities in locker rooms. What is it about this Patriots team that has you playing in a Super Bowl?

MM: I think I said this best when I signed here: You don’t need superstars to win football games. You just need a bunch of guys willing to lay it on the line. And that’s what we have.

Everyone says our schedule is easy. Football is not easy! You go out there and let someone hit you for 20-plus weeks straight. That’s why it’s our greatest sport. Look at some of the playoff teams this year. The Bears last year were a three- or four-win team, the Patriots [had] four wins last year. It doesn’t matter the schedule, you still gotta put that ball between the lines.

The difference with the locker room this year is we have a great mixture of veteran guys and young guys that are collaborating. Stefon Diggs has been an unsung hero for us. Mack Hollins, the glue to our room. There’s a number of veteran guys with juice left in their tank and then the young guys. Kudos to the front office for hitting on all the free agency guys. Craig Woodson, our starting safety, a fourth rounder, he’s played about every snap this season. Elijah Ponder, an undrafted defensive end, he’s one of the leading rookies in sacks. These are guys that a lot of people might’ve written off, and now they’re here on the biggest stage.

MH: We're days away from the Super Bowl. You’ve dreamt of playing in this game since you were a kid. What are your emotions as game day approaches?

MM: It’s been crazy because I’ve been trying to figure it out. I told myself this yesterday: "Morgan, you’re so ungrateful right now." Because I don't have any feelings toward it right now. Coach Vrabel said it best after we knew we were going to the Super Bowl: "If you lose this game, it will feel like someone stabbed you in the heart." It has taken me 12 years to get here, and I’m blessed to be here. But right now, I have to treat it like a regular game.

I have three sons, and they are super excited. They’re on the plane on the way here now and I had to say, "Look dudes, I know you’re excited, but I still have a job to do." No one wants to come to this game to lose. I know the emotions will explode on the sideline when the National Anthem is going, so I’ve been trying to keep my composure and who I need to be right now. We say, "Don’t change the mac-n-cheese on Thanksgiving Day." In other words, don’t be adding recipes. I’m trying to keep the same the same and be level-headed.
