# A Certified Sham

God bless the Internet. Where else can you forge a new career as a personal trainer on your lunch hour? One recent Friday morning, I signed on to expertrating.com, a "trusted name in online testing and certification," and paid the $49.99 fee. My plan was to cram for a week and take the test. But as I downloaded the course chapters, I decided to try to replace "a week" with "while the rest of the chapters are printing out."

[Health reveals just how](https://mendstride.snaketongueofficial.com/)

At 11:40 a.m., I began the test. The site promised 40 questions in 45 minutes, but I had lunch plans, so I aimed to finish by noon.

Twenty-five minutes later, I was a proven fitness superstar. My overall score of 68 percent was well over what I needed to pass--50 percent. I scored 100 percent on the nutrition, weight-loss, first-aid, and management-skills questions. Sadly, I mustered only a 17 percent on exercise routines and techniques.

Within days of my certificate's arrival ($55 for 4-day delivery), I had an interview with a national health-club franchise. "We don't recognize that degree," the manager said, "but as long as you pledge to get certified within 90 days, you're fine." He said it would take only a day (and an extra $100). Either way, he said, I could train clients as soon as I started. See you at the squat rack!
