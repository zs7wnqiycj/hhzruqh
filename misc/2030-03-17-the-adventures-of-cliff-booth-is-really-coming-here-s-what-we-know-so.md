A quasi-sequel to Quentin Tarantino's 2019 hit Once Upon a Time In Hollywood, the story goes that Tarantino had originally written a script with plans of it being his 10th and final film—but then abandoned the project. In came Brad Pitt, who played the titular stuntman Cliff Booth (and won an Oscar for his efforts) in Once Upon a Time, who loved the idea and got Tarantino's approval to see if he could make things work with David Fincher behind the camera instead. Fincher, who'd previously directed Pitt in modern classics like Se7en and Fight Club, was in, and Netflix (with whom Fincher has an exclusive deal) was on board.

That's a lot of names. The idea of Tarantino and Fincher—two of the great directors to emerge from the 1990s—teaming up simply feels like the kind of thing that doesn't and wouldn't happen. It's a 'What if?' game for movie buffs. If this was sports, we'd say it's like a video game. But no—this is happening, and during Super Bowl LX we got the first hint that it's really happening: A one-minute-long teaser that didn't include any on-screen plug for the very famous writer and the very famous director, a release date, or even a title.

And honestly? That's for the best. The fact that Netflix was willing to release a teaser that's so vague—it's got a scratchy, dirty, '70s aesthetic—and doesn't quickly try to sell its audience on the brand names and talent behind it really proves that the streaming giant is supremely confident that the movie itself is good enough to stand on its own, even with some risky lead marketing.

It's fair to question whether or not Cliff Booth will be anywhere near as good as Once Upon a Time was. Especially in our age of non-stop sequels, prequels, and all sorts of attempts to cash in on that thing you remember, it's hard not to respect some well-earned skepticism. Tarantino is now coming up on 7 years since his last movie was in theaters (and was most recently in the news for randomly and wrongly bad-mouthing a beloved actor), and Fincher's Netflix deal has made it so that his last movie with any sort of countable box office total was 2014's Gone Girl. That's a lot of time passed. (Fincher's major Netflix projects—the TV series Mindhunter along with films Mank and The Killer—are all very well done, but, as with all Netflix releases, we really don't have much empirical data to gauge how much these things really connected with audiences). There's also the fact that sometimes something with this much going on solely in its premise and make-up might turn out to be a big old mess.

But it's also OK to sometimes just place trust in the names involved. Fincher has shown a remarkable talent in the past for taking a script from a larger-than-life writer (i.e. The Social Network and Aaron Sorkin) and funneling it directly through his sleek, compelling, one-of-a-kind lens to make something special. Tarantino is, well, Tarantino—his stories are always going to be their specific brand of big and splashy. But this also isn't the first time one of his scripts has been handed over to another director; Tony Scott made his True Romance in 1993, and Oliver Stone made a version of his Natural Born Killers script in 1994. Even if the movie for whatever reason doesn't work out, it's hard to defeat the curiosity of seeing what happens when two distinct voices and visions have to find a way to meld into one.

There's also the Pitt aspect of things. The 62-year-old star has been no stranger to controversy in recent years, which certainly plays an impact in how many perceive his movie star persona in 2026 compared to, say, 7-10 years ago. But as far as the movies themselves go, he has a tendency to pick projects that range from pretty good to very good as a baseline. 2024's Wolfs was a miss, but last year's F1 was the big-budget adventure that was promised, and came away with a Best Picture nomination at the Academy Awards to prove it. Pitt's weakest collaboration with Fincher—2008's The Curious Case of Benjamin Button—is far from a disaster; It earned 13 Oscar nominations and won three of them. It's hard to imagine another project between the two amounting to much less.

The Cliff Booth trailer is only a minute long, but it tells us all we really need to know. Cliff has become something of a legend after helping his buddy/boss Rick Dalton (Leonardo DiCaprio) fight off the Manson Family intruders at the end of Once Upon a Time, and now he's getting into even more adventures, presumably in the stuntman world. Elizabeth Debicki (who starred in a similar period project with 2024's MaXXXine), Yahya Abdul Mateen II, and Scott Caan are among others in the cast, while Timothy Olyphant reprises his role from Once Upon a Time as actor James Stacy.
