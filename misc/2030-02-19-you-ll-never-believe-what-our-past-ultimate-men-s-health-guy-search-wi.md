# You'll Never Believe What Our Past Ultimate Men's Health Guy Search Winners Are Up To Now

We've seen a lot of impressive dudes since we started the Ultimate Men's Health Guy Search three years ago. Our first winner, Noah Galloway, inspired us with his story of overcoming serious physical injuries and depression through fitness. Tim Boniface, 2015's winner, was the Kentucky firefighter committed to helping his fellow firefighters live healthier lives by changing their diets and exercising. And you'll remember our most recent winner, Jed, as the emergency-medical-physician-slash-U.S.-Army-Ranger with a serious commitment to fitness.

Then there are the finalists, all with equally incredible stories that made selecting the winners such a tough choice. Here's a recap of our past winners and finalists to give you some inspiration for your Ultimate Men's Health Guy Search entry this year. Let's just say, the contestants have a lot to live up to.

2014 Winner: Noah Galloway

Noah Galloway was the winner of the inaugural search for the Ultimate Men's Health Guy back in 2014. Galloway was on tour in Iraq with the U.S. Army when the Humvee he was driving hit a roadside bomb. While Galloway survived, he woke up missing both his left arm and leg, and plunged into depression after he returned home. Then one day, he looked up at a mirror and realized he didn't like what was looking back at him. Galloway quit drinking and rebuilt himself with fitness, and has since vowed help others struggling. Galloway works with Operation Enduring Warrior, an organization that helps wounded soldiers, and has started his own charity, the No Excuses Charity Fund, which focuses on bringing fitness into the lives of others. He also was in Dancing With The Stars in 2015, winning third place in the competition. Since his Men's Health cover appearance, Galloway has written a book and is a highly sought-after public speaker.

2014 Finalist: Finny Akers

Finny Akers was 21 years old when tragedy struck his family— his father had killed his mother and then killed himself, leaving Akers and his young siblings, at the time ages 10 and 9, orphans. Akers was just about to start college, but instead took on a father/brother role for his brother and sister. Akers, who was courted by DI colleges to play lacrosse but ultimately gave it up to help his siblings, used fitness to help him through it all. Today, he works as a manager for What Goes Around Comes Around and continues to spread his love of fitness through his Instagram.

2014 Finalist: Kavan Lake

Kavan Lake is a Major in the U.S. Marine Corps, a father to two daughters and former coach of the Naval Academy's sprint football team. He grew up without a father and with very little on his back when he and his mother moved to New York City from Jamaica when he was young— literally. He rotated through two sets of clothing through high school and at times didn't have any shoes to wear. But he was able to put himself through college and rise up in the Marine Corps.

2015 Winner: Tim Boniface

[You'll Never](https://beautybiometrics.hbsnzj.com/blog/9200710545.html)

Tim Boniface was the winner of 2015's Ultimate Men's Health Guy Search. Boniface is a firefighter who left a career in banking for to pursue his lifelong passion for firefighting. When Boniface learned that the number one cause of deaths for on-duty firefighters was cardiac arrest, he decided to do something about it. He committed himself to fitness and making healthier lifestyle choices and does his best to help other firefighters do the same.

2015 Finalist: Aydian Dowling

Aydian Dowling was the first transgender person to be selected for a finalist slot in the Ultimate Men's Health Guy Search. Dowling, hailing from Eugene, Oregon, grew up a female before undergoing hormone injections and breast removal surgery starting at age 21 to transition to a male. Dowling won the most reader's votes for the 2015 competition and today runs an international clothing company that donates a portion of its funds to helping transgenders undergo gender transformation surgeries. Dowling also runs and a popular YouTube channel and helps other train other transgenders fitness and body building in his hometown.

2015 Finalist: Danny Farrar

Danny Farrar served in Iraq with the U.S. Army Reserve and returned "changed," turning to drug and alcohol abuse before attempting suicide. He woke up in the hospital and knew he had to do something. That was when Farrar found MMA, and his life was changed. Farrar also helped found the nonprofit Platoon 22, which is dedicated to helping reduce the number of veteran suicides by providing tools to veterans and helping to research causes, prevention and treatment of PTSD.

2015 Finalist: Omari Grey

Omari Grey was cut from his college basketball team and lost his scholarship before moving with his wife to the Middle East for 10 years where he taught math to children. It was there that Grey "grew up," the experience opening his eyes to the poverty of the world. Today, Grey is a personal trainer, father to nine children and teaches calculus at a Washington, D.C. area charter school.

2015 Finalist: Adam Wheeler

Adam Wheeler is an Olympic medalist, SWAT team member, father and Brazilian Jiu Jitsu coach fiercely committed to fitness. Wheeler represented Team USA in the 2008 Beijing Summer Olympics and brought back a bronze medal. He retired from the sport after his Olympic debut to pursue his passion for law enforcement, joining the Colorado Springs Police Department, eventually joining their SWAT team.

2016 Winner: Jedidiah Ballard

Our most recent winner is an emergency medical physician and U.S. Army Ranger who served as a battalion surgeon with the Rangers in Afghanistan. Ballard makes time for working out in-between a hectic schedule of E.R. shifts and teaching emergency medicine at Augusta University in Georgia. He also frequently volunteers his time by flying to Peru and Panama to teach doctors how to use ultrasound machines to identify blood clots and other serious medical conditions. He also participates in (and often, wins) obstacle course races and is an avid hiker, one time hiking Mount Hood by himself in a pair of $20 boots from Walmart.

After winning our search last year, he's looking to win over The Bachelorette's heart this season, which premieres Monday, May 22. Does Ballard have what it takes to earn a rose from Rachel Lindsay? We'll have to tune in to find out.

2016 Finalist: Otis Hooper

Lt. Col. Otis Hooper is a U.S. Air Force pilot and dad to four boys, who was prompted to change his lifestyle after a "Dad Talk." He was talking to his boys about the importance of eating right and working hard when one of them poked his stomach and asked, "What about you?" That made Hooper realize that his kids were always watching, and he wanted to become a better role model for them. Hooper enlisted the help of one of his friends, who was a personal trainer, and the rest is hard work and a lot of sweat. Hooper decided to start entering physique competitions and, at 39 years old, is not showing any signs of slowing down.

2016 Finalist: Tommy Tucker

Tommy Tucker served in the Marine Corps and now works as a security contractor, protecting government embassies all over the world. He woke up one day and didn't feel right. Some doctor's visits and a CT scan later revealed Tucker had inoperable stage IV neuroendocrine cancer. Tucker underwent rounds of chemotherapy before turning to Eastern medicine, where instead of fighting the disease he focuses on embracing it and enabling his body to heal itself through diet, exercise and meditation. He's also a fitness instructor and his website offers workouts, inspiration and advice to readers.
