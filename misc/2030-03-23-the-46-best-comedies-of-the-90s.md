# The 46 Best Comedies of the ‘90s

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

Everybody loves to laugh. And luckily, there are a whole lot of movies that can help us do that. Sometimes we love a show that we can really sink our teeth into—think the many seasons of something like The Office, 30 Rock, or even Seinfeld. But other times we just want something that we can start at 8:00 and be done with by 9:30. it's the glory of a good old fashioned movie: whether you wind up liking it or hating it, by the time the end credits start to roll, it's not the biggest commitment you're going to be making this week.

Each decade has its winners and its losers, but the '90s certainly have a strong case for having the greatest catalogue of comedy movies that are just good. We're talking movies that will have you cracking up, and also movies that you may not be literally slapping your knee, but rather something you recognize in your head, silently, as clever, and then find yourself thinking about later that night. And then again later that week. And then again, a full month or even year down the road. Some of these movies can get pretty deep, we're saying—looking at you, The Truman Show and Being John Malkovich.

There's a lot of variance on this list, for sure. Something being funny—comedies in general—aren't always dumb or gross slapstick humor. Don't get us wrong—there's plenty of merit in that too; this list has a whole lot of Jim Carrey and Adam Sandler in their prime for that purpose. But we also we also wanted to include some movies that could certainly fall under the comedy umbrella, but are more intended as social satire or perhaps a dramedy than a traditional gut-busting comedy.

So, without further ado, here are 46 funny comedies—of varying degrees and intent—from the '90s that are 100% worth watching.

The Big Lebowski (1998)

The Big Lebowski is a cult classic for all the best reasons; it's good enough on first viewing (it's impossible not to appreciate Jeff Bridges, John Goodman, and Steve Buscemi, among others), but it's a movie that really just gets better and better with each viewing. The movie is a low-stakes screwball caper, if you had to put a genre on it, but it's the Coen Brothers repeatedly show their utter skill at being oddly hilarious in a lot of dry and totally unexpected ways, from the writing to the characters' body language. I can't speak for anyone but myself, but I'll be watching this movie a couple times a year for the rest of my life.

Dumb & Dumber (1994)

The title really says it all, huh? Jeff Daniels and Jim Carrey are quite possibly the greatest comedy duo of the '90s, playing two dim-witted best friends who find themselves in a dangerous situation—and have no idea what that situation even is. Lots of laughs guaranteed (and ignore the sequel/prequel/etc).

Small Soldiers (1998)

Curveball! This movie is mostly forgotten, but it's actually a really sharp and fun mix of a teen-focused adventure movie and a satire of (cliche, yes) capitalism/consumerism shortcomngs. The basic hook? State-of-the-art toys end up getting implanted with state-of-the-art military technology and AI. Whoops! Except in this situation, the G.I. Joe-type toys are the bad guys, and the Outcasts are the good guys. The very fun cast also includes David Cross, Jay Mohr, Denis Leary, and the late Phil Hartman, while the toys are voiced by Tommy Lee Jones, Frank Langella, and many other notable names.

The Cable Guy (1996)

Jim Carrey got a big payday for The Cable Guy, which was a major curveball of a movie at the time. Coming off comedies like The Mask, Dumb and Dumber, and Ace Ventura: Pet Detective, he could've done just about anything. He chose to do this dark comedy, playing the titular character, desperate to make a new friend (Matthew Broderick). Directed by Ben Stiller and produced by Judd Apatow, there was a real wrecking crew involved with this one.

BASEketball (1998)

You may also be surprised to see BASEketball, a largely forgotten sports comedy from the creators of South Park, on this list. But predictably crude and vulgar humor aside, the movie is actually a pretty sharp (and prescient in a lot of ways) take on the modern state of the sports world. It's just fun! Check it out if you want a silly, goofy ride that's at least a little bit smarter than it seems.

Tommy Boy (1995)

Chris Farley became a comedy legend in the '90s, and while he may be best known for his time on Saturday Night Live, it's hard to argue that Tommy Boy was his most successful and beloved movie. He and David Spade made for a great team, as they tried to save a family-owned automotive business. It simply doesn't get better than Farley singing "Fat guy in a little coat..."

Black Sheep (1996)

If you liked Tommy Boy, you can get basically more of the same—right down to the Farley and Spade pairing—with Black Sheep. Fun flick.

Waiting For Guffman (1997)

Waiting For Guffman is the first time Chrisotpher Guest directed one of his famous mockumentary films (This Is Spinal Tap came prior, but was directed by Rob Reiner), and it certainly sets the tone for future movies to come. This quick movie (only 1 hour and 23 minutes) is about a hilarious small midwestern town's stage production. Guest stars, along with several of his regular pals: Eugene Levy, Cathrine O'Hara, Bob Balaban, Parker Posey, and Fred Willard. You'll never forget the moving tribute to stools.

Office Space (1999)

The Office is great, but the droll workplace comedy of Office Space came even earlier. This is just a classic film of disenchantment at work, and getting back at the people who drive you crazy. Gary Cole plays the asshole boss of your nightmares, among other perfect portrayals.

Rush Hour (1998)

One of the great buddy comedy flicks. Rush Hour pairs Chris Tucker's wise-cracking mouth with Jackie Chan's underrated comedic timing (and amazing martial arts moves) for a movie that's just as exciting as it is hilarious. It ended up spawning two sequels, and, hell, I don't think too many people would complain if they decided to give us at least one more.

Rushmore (1998)

While their titles are similar, I don't think there are two movies on this list anymore different than Rushmore and Rush Hour. Rushmore is Wes Anderson's movie about a young student (Jason Schwartzman, in his breakout role) who's precarious far beyond his years, competing for the romance of one of his teachers with—who else?—Bill Murray.

The Wedding Singer (1998)

We'll be hearing quite a bit more from Mr. Sandler on this list, but it's best to get started with The Wedding Singer, which is his best movie. It's got heart, it's got laughs, and it's got some really great '80s nostalgia moments. It's also got Steve Buscemi as a very drunk best man. Excellence.

Dazed and Confused (1993)

Part of what makes Dazed and Confused such a great '90s movie is that it's...not even set in the '90s. Since this 1993 movie was depicting the last day of school in 1976, it has literally not aged poorly by even one day. This movie—which marked the film debut of Matthew McConaughey, in an iconic role—is basically perfect. Richard Linklater is one of our finest directors, and this is one of his masterworks.

Can't Hardly Wait (1998)

The best part of most teen comedy movies is the party scene. So Can't Hardly Wait decided to lean into that—and make the vast majority of the movie a big party scene. One of the best teen comedies of the '90s (or any other decade!).

Election (1999)

Election is such a sharp movie, a dark comedy that doesn't need anyone to die or be in danger of real physical harm to have real stakes. Matthew Broderick plays a likable high school teacher who finally meets his match—a teen with endless ambition played by Reese Witherspoon.

White Men Can't Jump (1992)

This movie pairs two bona fide '90s stars—Wesley Snipes and Woody Harrelson—together as basketball hustlers with an up and down relationship. And Rosie Perez finds a great role with a character who really, really, wants to go on Jeopardy!.

American Pie (1999)

A lot of the comedy in American Pie has aged, um, not quite admirably. But at its core, it's a product of its time, a movie about a bunch of horned-up dudes who are pretty desperate to get laid for the first time before heading off to college. It's funny, started perhaps the most notable franchise of the crude comedy genre, and has a really great pop-punk soundtrack. Always a fun—and funny—watch.

10 Things I Hate About You (1999)

Based loosely on Shakespeare's The Taming of the Shrew, 10 Things I Hate About You finds the late, great Heath Ledger at perhaps his most charming. While he's come to be known best for his roles as The Joker in The Dark Knight or in Brokeback Mountain, it's worth remembering just how charming he could be in movies like this one. He can serenade someone on the football field bleachers with the best of 'em! Joseph Gordon-Levitt and Julia Stiles round out the movie's fun cast.

Austin Powers: International Man of Mystery (1997)

The first Austin Powers tells his story in the most novel fashion: he's a groovy, shagadelic, 007 type. And he's super funny. So many lines that have been quoted for, well, literally 24 years at this point. If you somehow have never seen this, you've got to watch. Still funny!
