What can cause tailbone pain when sitting down and getting up, and what are the treatment options?

Tailbone pain that flares up when sitting down or standing up is known as coccydynia. Common causes include falls or direct injury, prolonged sitting on hard surfaces, childbirth, repetitive strain, and, less often, infections or tumors near the coccyx. Symptoms typically include sharp or aching pain at the base of the spine, tenderness to touch, and discomfort during bowel movements or intercourse. Treatment usually starts with conservative options: cushioned or wedge-shaped seats, NSAIDs like ibuprofen, targeted stretches, warm compresses, and physical therapy. Persistent cases may require corticosteroid injections or, rarely, surgery. What can cause waking up with heartburn in the middle of the night and how can it be managed?

Waking up with nighttime heartburn is often caused by eating too close to bedtime, which allows stomach acid to flow back into the esophagus while you're lying flat. To reduce symptoms, finish meals at least 3 hours before bed, elevate your head while sleeping, and avoid trigger foods like spicy or fatty dishes. Medications such as pantoprazole, a proton pump inhibitor, can also help by reducing stomach acid production. However, nighttime heartburn can sometimes signal a more serious issue like GERD, a hiatal hernia, or even cardiac concerns that mimic reflux. What causes the start and stop of urination?

Urination starts and stops through the coordinated action of nerves and muscles that control the bladder and urethra. The bladder wall (detrusor muscle) contracts to push urine out, while the urethral sphincters relax to allow flow. When urination ends, the sphincters tighten and the detrusor relaxes, stopping the stream. This process is regulated by signals between the brain, spinal cord, and pelvic nerves. If your urination pattern feels abnormal—such as frequent stopping and starting, weak flow, or difficulty emptying—it could signal an underlying issue like a urinary tract infection, enlarged prostate, or nerve dysfunction.
