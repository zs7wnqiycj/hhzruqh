Narcolepsy at work: what to know

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological disorder that causes excessive daytime sleepiness, sudden sleep attacks, and sometimes cataplexy—symptoms that can make workdays challenging. People with narcolepsy can manage symptoms at work by requesting workplace accommodations (such as scheduled nap breaks, flexible hours, or remote work) and by being aware of how external factors, like COVID-19 restrictions or changes to routine, may worsen fatigue and disrupt sleep patterns.

Managing narcolepsy at work can be challenging, but there are ways to make it more manageable. One way is through workplace accommodations. This might include having a flexible schedule, taking short naps during breaks, or having a quiet space to rest. These accommodations can help people with narcolepsy stay alert and productive.

Another important aspect to consider is how external factors, such as COVID-19-related restrictions, can impact narcolepsy symptoms. Changes in routine or increased stress levels might make symptoms worse. It is helpful to be aware of these factors and communicate with employers to find supportive solutions.

By understanding these strategies and being proactive in seeking accommodations, individuals with narcolepsy can better manage their symptoms and maintain their work performance.
