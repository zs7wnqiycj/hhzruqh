Halloween isn’t for another few months, but lucky for us, ghosts are always in season—and there are plenty of movies to prove it. Ghost movies are a special category of horror. There’s something about the paranormal that sets itself apart from monsters, slashers, and other sub-genres of the like. With ghost films, the director has the intricate job of working with the unseen, and proving that sometimes what we can’t see is the most frightening thing of all.

Why do we flock to the theater for movies that should have us running in the other direction? According to Dr. Katherine Brownlowe, an assistant professor in the neurology and psychiatry departments at The Ohio State University, having to calm yourself down after watching a scary movie is chemically pleasing for your brain. Who knew? So let’s give these movies the credit they deserve. (After all, in the entire history of the Oscar awards, just six horror films have been nominated.)

If there's one creature that'll always scare us, it's a ghost. Something about being able to communicate with those who are dead and gone (or evil spirits who look to mess with the living) is far more unsettling than monsters you can kill or any horror made of natural means. Ghosts will never really get old.

Here’s a list of the 65 ghost movies that make creaky floorboards leave a knot in your stomach, and turn innocent shadows into haunting silhouettes.

Addicted to TV? Here’s one movie that'll ensure you don’t look at a screen for a while. New house, same hauntings. Did you really think the demons wouldn’t put up a fight? Although Dalton has been rescued from what the characters call “The Further,” evil continues to plague the family. Beetlejuice (1988)

Beetlejuice, Beetlejuice, Beetlejuice! This is definitely a light-hearted classic to watch with your friends, even if it’s only for the beloved Banana Boat dinner scene. Who could forget?

The Shining (1980)

Based on the book by the modern father of horror, Stephen King, this movie sends a family to a remote hotel in Colorado, where things start to spiral out of control and out of this realm. Is the family going crazy from seclusion? Or is there more to the chaos than meets the eye?

The Sixth Sense (1999)

Directed by M. Night Shyamalan, The Sixth Sense brings together Malcolm (Bruce Willis), a child psychologist, and a little boy with a unique and chilling gift: He can see and talk with the deceased. In an attempt to help the boy, we discover something about Malcolm that not even he knew.

The Boy (2016)

You thought you were afraid of dolls before? How about now? When a nanny takes a new job looking after a young boy, she is shocked to find that the “child” is really a porcelain doll. After their real son died in a house fire, the parents have taken care of the doll, Brahms, like their real boy. The kicker? Brahms moves around when they’re asleep. Could this be the ghost of their son? Or the work of something even scarier?

The Invisible Man (2020)

Okay, so not your typical ghost movie, but maybe something even more hair-raising. When her abusive ex-boyfriend takes his own life, a woman (Elisabeth Moss) doesn’t believe he’s really gone. Is she being haunted by a ghost, or an evil that was created in a lab?

Mama (2013)

Giving new meaning to the phrase “mom is always watching,” Mama shows two sisters' attempts to reintegrate into society after their parents' deaths led them to spend five years alone in the woods. But something seems to have followed them out.

A Tale of Two Sisters (2003)

This Korean film is an all-in-one horror and psychological thriller that will leave you with your jaw on the floor. Try to keep up with the twists and turns of this unique ghost story.
