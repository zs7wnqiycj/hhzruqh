What Exactly Causes Bunions?

Unfortunately, there is no clear consensus on an exact cause for bunions. There are genetic factors as well as physical factors. Wearing shoes that constrict your toes or force excessive stress on the balls of your feet can create or exacerbate the growth of bunions. Heeled shoes with a narrow toe box—like loafers or dress shoes—are especially problematic.

Genetic factors like rheumatoid arthritis and joint inflammation are known to combine with walking habits and footwear choices, resulting in bunions. However, wearing constricting shoes is not guaranteed to give you bunions.

How Can the Correct Shoes Help with Bunions?

The bump protruding from the base of the big toe—the actual bunion—is often combined with joint pain within the big toe and the two symptoms can compound foot pain if not managed properly. You want to cut down on friction along the sides of your toes and you want as much support and cushioning for the balls of your feet. Controlling these factors will limit swelling which is the root cause of bunion pain.

Both of our experts explained that surgery is the only method of curing bunions, but wearing the correct shoes can prevent growth and alleviate pain. As Dr. Sharkey explained, “Choosing a shoe that fits your foot well and is comfortable to wear will allow you to perform your activities without pain. Shoes themselves cannot reverse or treat bunion deformities but choosing the right shoe can make living with a bunion easier.”

What Features Should You Look For?

The best shoes for bunions have a wider toe box and softer upper fabric to help with the bump, as well as ample support in the sole, little-to-no heel, and overall flexibility to help with joint pain.

Running and walking shoes are a good start and the more stability and cushioning involved, the better. You do not necessarily need to buy wide fit shoes, as long as the toe box is wide. Sandals are great for eliminating friction on the growth but can lack support for joint pain, so look for sandals with ample support.

Dress shoes are where things get tricky. Dr. McEneaney pointed out that the stiffness of leather and the tendency for men’s dress shoes to have pointed toes eliminate many options for those with bunions. “I tend to recommend to my patients with bunions dress shoes that are more rounded at the front instead of pointed, or a style that’s almost squared out.” He told us, pointing out that wingtips and cap toe shoes are best avoided in favor of rounded or square toe shoes. Dr. Sharkey recommends avoiding any shoes with heels greater than 2 inches.
