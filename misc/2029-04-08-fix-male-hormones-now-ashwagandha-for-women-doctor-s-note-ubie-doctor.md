How Ashwagandha Balances Male Hormones in Women, According to a Doctor

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an adaptogenic herb long used in Ayurvedic medicine to help the body adapt to stress. In recent years, researchers have explored its impact on hormones—particularly how it can influence testosterone (often called a "male" hormone) in women. Below, we explain what current studies and clinical experience reveal about ashwagandha and testosterone for women, how it works, its benefits, and practical tips for safe use.

Understanding Testosterone in Women

Although testosterone is often labeled a "male" hormone, it plays a vital role in women's health:

Sexual health & libidoSupports sexual desire, arousal and satisfaction.

Bone & muscle strengthPromotes lean muscle mass and bone density.

Mood & energyHelps maintain motivation, focus, and well-being.

MetabolismAids in regulating fat distribution and insulin sensitivity.

However, both low and high testosterone can cause symptoms:

Low levels: fatigue, low libido, depressed mood, reduced muscle mass.

High levels (as in PCOS): irregular periods, excessive hair growth, acne, weight gain.

Balancing testosterone—rather than simply raising or lowering it—supports overall hormonal harmony.

How Ashwagandha Works on Hormones

Ashwagandha's main active compounds are withanolides, which appear to modulate the body's stress response and hormone production via the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis and the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis:

Stress reduction• Lowers cortisol, the "stress hormone."• Less cortisol often leads to more balanced sex hormone production.

HPA axis modulation• Normalizes signaling between brain and adrenal glands.• Reduces overdrive that can throw sex hormones out of balance.

HPG axis support• Helps regulate signals that control ovarian hormone production.• Can support healthy testosterone levels when they are too low.

Clinical Evidence: What Research Shows

While most early studies focused on men, emerging data and small clinical trials in women point to several benefits:

Stress & cortisolA randomized trial found that 300 mg of ashwagandha extract twice daily for 60 days significantly reduced serum cortisol levels by as much as 27%. Lower cortisol often translates to more balanced testosterone production.

PCOS and androgen levelsIn a pilot study of women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), ashwagandha supplementation (300 mg twice daily) for 12 weeks improved menstrual regularity and reduced serum DHEA-S (an androgen precursor).

Thyroid supportHypothyroid women taking 600 mg/day of ashwagandha saw improved thyroid hormone levels, indirectly supporting better overall hormone balance, including androgens.

Mood & energySeveral trials in both men and women have shown improved mood, reduced anxiety and better energy—factors that indirectly influence sexual hormones.

Potential Benefits for Women

By helping to balance testosterone and other hormones, ashwagandha may deliver:

Increased libido and sexual satisfaction

Reduced symptoms of PCOS (acne, hirsutism, irregular periods)

Improved mood, reduced anxiety and better stress resilience

Enhanced muscle tone and bone health

Better sleep quality (important for hormone regulation)

Dosage & How to Choose a Quality Supplement

When selecting and dosing ashwagandha:

Look for standardized extracts with at least 5% withanolides.

Common dosage range:• 300–600 mg per day, divided into 1–2 doses.• Some studies use up to 1,000 mg/day, but start low and increase as tolerated.

Take with meals to reduce the chance of stomach upset.

Avoid combining multiple adaptogens at once until you gauge your response.

Safety, Side Effects & Precautions

Ashwagandha is generally well tolerated, but be aware of:

Possible side effects• Mild stomach upset, diarrhea or nausea• Headache or drowsiness in some people

Interactions• Sedatives or sleep medications (may enhance drowsiness)• Thyroid hormone replacement (monitor if you have thyroid disease)• Immunosuppressants (ashwagandha can boost immune activity)

When to avoid• Pregnancy and breastfeeding (not enough safety data)• Autoimmune conditions like lupus or rheumatoid arthritis• If you have an upcoming surgery (may lower blood pressure and increase sedation)

Always discuss supplements with your healthcare provider, especially if you have existing health conditions or take prescription medications.
