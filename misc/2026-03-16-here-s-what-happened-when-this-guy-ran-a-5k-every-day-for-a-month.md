# Here's What Happened When This Guy Ran a 5K Every Day for a Month

For fitness buffs, running a 5K may sound pretty easy, especially if you aren’t pushing for time. But running a 5K for every single day for a month? Now that’s just a tad more intense.

Fitness model, trainer and YouTuber Alex Crockford documented his self-imposed challenge of running a 5K every day for a month, which is a little over three miles every single day for 30 days. Even though he’s an exercise pro, Crockford learned a lot along the way that will probably help your running routine.

Though Crockford has run marathons in the past and keeps up with a steady exercise regimen, when he started the challenge, he was coming off about seven months of not running at all. On his first day, the run took him about 29 minutes. He notes that his knees felt “uncertain” and legs were unconditioned, even though he consistently weight trains in the gym.

Over the first 10 days, Crockford said he was getting “faster and lighter,” though he wasn’t running with the intention of decreasing his running time. But Crockford’s time got down to 25 minutes within those first 10 days without even really trying. The pro didn’t give up weight training during this challenge, finding that his times were obviously slower after a leg day. On his last day of the challenge, Crockford intentionally pushed himself “a little harder” than usual and got his time down to 23 minutes.

Crockford said he had been dealing with a troublesome knee all year, and was at first worried that the challenge could cause further pain and stress to the joint. Interestingly, he reported running actually made his knee feel better, inferring the exercise acted as a little rehab for the weakened joint.

[Every Day for Month](https://innate-signal.jcesqc8.com/)

Though Crockford adds running in the cold mornings wasn’t easy on his mind or body, it did lead to results. “In the process of one month of running, I lost about two kilograms,” Crockford says. That’s about five pounds to us Americans. The YouTuber says balancing his nutrition with his new upped cardio helped him get lean without losing muscle, which was his ultimate goal.

Even though Crockford completed the challenge, he adds that he doesn’t recommend it as the challenge doesn’t leave room for rest and recovery. In fact, Crockford says he took off Day 18 of the challenge completely due to his own need to rest from illness. But that doesn’t make his completion of the challenge any less valid. It simply makes him a smart athlete.

Even though he doesn’t recommend the challenge, Crockford says he does recommend running consistently. And he plans to keep running in his weekly exercise routine—but just two to three times a week.

“The reason I took on this challenge is because, of course, I wanted to get better at running,” Crockford says in the video. “I wanted to see what would happen to me physically and in terms of fitness in the period of time. But I also wanted to motivate and inspire the people that have been following me to see what they can do every single day.” Mission accomplished.
