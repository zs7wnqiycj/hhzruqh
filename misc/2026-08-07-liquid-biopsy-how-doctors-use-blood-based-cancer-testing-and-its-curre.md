Liquid Biopsy: How Doctors Use Blood-Based Cancer Testing and Its Current Limitations

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Liquid biopsy is a minimally invasive blood test that detects cancer-related material—such as circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), circulating tumor cells (CTCs), and exosomes—from a simple blood draw. This advanced technology enables earlier cancer detection, real-time monitoring of treatment response, and molecular profiling to guide targeted therapies, all without the risks of traditional tissue biopsies.

However, liquid biopsies have key limitations. Sensitivity and specificity vary by test, lab standardization is still evolving, and cost or insurance coverage can limit accessibility—factors that may affect how reliably results guide care decisions.

Explanation

Liquid biopsy cancer tests have emerged as a promising tool in oncology. Unlike traditional tissue biopsies, which require invasive surgery or needle procedures to remove a tumor sample, liquid biopsies analyze cancer-related material circulating in the blood. This approach offers the potential for earlier detection, real-time monitoring of treatment response, and insights into tumor evolution—while minimizing discomfort and risk.

What Is a Liquid Biopsy Cancer Test?

A liquid biopsy cancer test examines biomarkers shed by tumors into the bloodstream. Key targets include:

Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA): Fragments of DNA released by dying cancer cells.

Circulating tumor cells (CTCs): Intact cells that break away from primary or metastatic sites.

Exosomes and microvesicles: Tiny particles carrying proteins, RNA, or DNA from cancer cells.

Clinical Applications of the Liquid Biopsy Cancer Test

Liquid biopsies are being integrated into several areas of cancer care:

Early Detection and Screening

Potential to identify cancerous changes before symptoms appear.

Molecular Profiling for Targeted Therapy

Identification of actionable mutations (e.g., EGFR, KRAS, BRAF) that inform drug selection.

Enables treatment personalization when tissue biopsy is impractical.

Monitoring Treatment Response

Quantitative tracking of ctDNA levels can reflect tumor burden in real time.

May reveal early signs of drug resistance before radiologic progression.

Detection of Minimal Residual Disease (MRD)

After surgery or chemotherapy, extremely low levels of ctDNA can indicate residual cancer cells.

Supports decisions about adjuvant therapy intensity.

Early Recurrence Surveillance

Rising ctDNA or new mutations can flag relapse sooner than imaging.

Promotes timely intervention.

Advantages of Liquid Biopsy Cancer Tests

Minimally invasive, requiring only a blood draw rather than a tissue sample.

Repeatable at frequent intervals to monitor disease dynamics.

Potential to capture genetic heterogeneity from multiple tumor sites.

Faster turnaround times compared to some tissue-based assays.

Reduced procedural risks (bleeding, infection, anaesthesia).

Current Limitations

While promising, liquid biopsy cancer tests have important constraints:

1. Sensitivity and Detection Limits

Very early-stage cancers or small tumor burdens may shed insufficient ctDNA or CTCs to detect reliably.

False-negative results can occur, particularly in cancers with low shedding (e.g., certain brain tumors).

2. Specificity and False Positives

Non-cancerous conditions (inflammation, benign clonal hematopoiesis) can produce mutations detectable in blood.

Differentiating tumor-derived alterations from background noise requires rigorous bioinformatics.

3. Standardization and Validation

Numerous platforms and protocols exist, but few are fully standardized.

Clinical utility varies by assay, cancer type, and intended use.

Lack of uniform guidelines can lead to inconsistent interpretation across laboratories.

4. Clinical Evidence and Guidelines

Robust evidence from large clinical trials is still emerging for many applications (especially screening).

Professional guidelines currently recommend specific uses (e.g., EGFR mutation testing in advanced lung cancer) but are cautious on others.

5. Cost and Accessibility

Advanced molecular assays can be expensive and may not be covered by all insurers.

Access to high-quality testing labs may be limited in some regions.

6. Turnaround Time and Logistics

Although faster than some tissue tests, shipping, processing, and analysis can still take days to weeks.

Delays may impact urgent treatment decisions.

Future Directions

Researchers and clinicians are working to overcome current limitations:

Enhanced Sensitivity: Ultra-deep sequencing and novel enrichment methods aim to detect even minute ctDNA fragments.

Multi-omics Approaches: Combining DNA, RNA, proteins, and epigenetic markers to improve accuracy.

Machine Learning Integration: Advanced algorithms to distinguish true positives from background noise.

Broader Clinical Trials: Large-scale studies to validate liquid biopsy cancer test use in screening, MRD detection, and early intervention.

Key Takeaways

Liquid biopsy cancer tests analyze tumor-derived materials in blood for diagnosis, monitoring, and therapy guidance.

They offer a minimally invasive alternative to tissue biopsies, with the potential for frequent sampling.

Current limitations include sensitivity in early disease, specificity challenges, standardization gaps, and cost.

Ongoing research aims to enhance accuracy, clinical utility, and accessibility.

Always discuss test results and treatment options with a qualified oncologist or specialist.

If you experience any concerning symptoms or receive abnormal test results, please speak to a doctor promptly. Your healthcare team can interpret liquid biopsy findings in the context of your overall health and guide appropriate care.
