Explanation

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an herbal adaptogen long used in Ayurvedic medicine. In recent years, practitioners and researchers have explored its potential role in enhancing physical performance. If you've heard about Ashwagandha and athletic power, you're not alone—doctors may recommend this supplement to support strength, recovery, and overall well-being. Here's what you need to know, based on credible studies and clinical insights.

1. What Makes Ashwagandha an Adaptogen?

Adaptogens are compounds that help the body resist diverse stressors—physical, chemical, or biological. Ashwagandha's adaptogenic properties stem from its bioactive components, notably withanolides. These compounds:

Balance the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis

Modulate cortisol (the stress hormone) levels

2. Cortisol Control and Muscle Health

High cortisol can inhibit muscle protein synthesis and accelerate protein breakdown—counterproductive for anyone aiming at strength gains. Studies suggest that ashwagandha:

Reduces serum cortisol by up to 27% in chronically stressed adults

Limits exercise-induced spikes in cortisol during intense workouts

May preserve lean muscle mass under prolonged physical or mental stress

When cortisol is kept in check, anabolic (muscle-building) processes face less resistance, making room for better recovery and gains.

3. Boosting Testosterone and Anabolic Markers

A 2015 randomized trial found a 14.7% increase in testosterone among healthy men taking 300 mg twice daily for 8 weeks.

Free testosterone levels rose even more dramatically, supporting greater muscle protein synthesis.

Improved hormonal profile often translates into enhanced athletic power, strength, and endurance.

While individual responses vary, these shifts can be meaningful for both recreational lifters and competitive athletes.

4. Enhanced Muscle Strength and Size

Several well-designed studies have evaluated ashwagandha's effect on strength and muscle mass:

47% greater increase in bench-press strength

38% greater gain in leg-extension strength

Significant boosts in muscle size compared to placebo

Participants also reported reduced exercise-induced muscle damage and faster recovery between sets.

These findings support the notion that pairing resistance training with ashwagandha may accelerate strength adaptations compared to training alone.

Research shows up to a 9% increase in VO₂ max after 8 weeks of supplementation at 600 mg per day.

Better oxygen consumption allows athletes to maintain higher intensities for longer.

7. Dosage and Standardization

Clinical trials typically use standardized extracts to ensure consistent withanolide content:

Common dosage: 300–600 mg per day, split into two doses

Standardization: Look for 5% withanolides (e.g., KSM-66® or Sensoril® brands)

Timing: Take with meals for better absorption and to minimize gastrointestinal discomfort

Always follow product labels and your doctor's recommendations.
