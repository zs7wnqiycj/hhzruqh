You can take on this mobility session anywhere you have space to spread out and move on the floor. The four movements don't require any special equipment either—just your bodyweight. You can perform the exercises first thing in the morning to get moving, after a strength training workout, or anytime you want to limber up. Rowe also says you can run through the series up to three times if you really want to work through your range of motion.

The Summer Shred 5-Minute Mobility Plan

World's Greatest Stretch

[for This Mobility Workout](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/9223803297.html)

Why: This exercise will help you loosen up your hips and thoracic spine. Make sure to move deliberately; don't rush.

How to Do It:

Start in a high plank position, with your hands stacked beneath your shoulders, palms on the floor.

Drive one knee up and plant your foot next to your same-side hand.

Lift that hand off the floor, then bend your arm and rotate in with your elbow down to the floor.

Keeping your other hand planted on the floor, arm straight, rotate your torso and reach up to the sky with your off arm. Turn your head to your eyes locked on your hand.

Reverse the movement to return to the start.

Sets and Reps: 45 seconds per side

Cat-Cow

Why: After rotating the thoracic spine with the previous move, this stretch will help you move through its whole range of motion, moving the vertebrae individually.

Get in a tabletop position (hands, knees, and feet on the ground), with the shoulders above your wrists. Your knees slightly wider than hip width.

Round your back as much as possible, while spreading your shoulder blades and bending in your neck as much as possible

Look up to slowly begin arching your back, taking two to three seconds to create as much spinal extension as possible while squeezing your shoulder blades.

Continue alternating between extension and flexion, moving for 2 to 3 seconds per each rep.

Dynamic Pigeon

Lift one leg off the floor, raising it up and reaching back to the endpoint of your range of motion.

Drive the knee forward, placing your bent leg on the floor in between your hands (as your mobility allows) with the outside of the foot on the floor.

Lower your torso down to the floor, as much as your mobility allows. Don't worry if you can't reach all the way down.

Table Top Bridge

Why: Rowe calls this his favorite, go-to movement to open up the chest and correct his posture. If you spend time sitting hunched over a desk, this stretch is for you.

Start sitting on the floor, with your palms on the ground at about shoulder-width apart and your hands facing out. Your feet should also be at about shoulder-width apart, with your feet flat on the floor.

Drive your hips up into extension. Hold the position, squeezing your abs and glutes. Keep your gaze straight ahead to maintain a neutral spine.

Lower back down to the starting position slowly.

Sets and Reps: 45 seconds.
