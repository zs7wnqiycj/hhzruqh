# ‘Yellowstone’ Fans Are Turning on John After the Latest Jamie Incident

It seemed like John wanted to resolved things with Jamie early into Yellowstone’s fourth season. After the attack on himself, Beth, and Kayce, John requested Jamie interview the prime suspect, a prisoner named Riggins. Kayce, too, thought Jamie should be entrusted with helping the Dutton family. John told Kayce that if Jamie was up for the task, he would meet with Jamie privately—meaning they would reconcile whatever differences they had.

Those differences are... confusing. For the entire run of the series, all John has done is abuse and manipulate Jamie. He took away his support for Jamie’s run at attorney general and turned him to a pariah on the ranch. Then Jamie became a real pariah after learning John had lied to him his whole life; Jamie was actually adopted—he is not a Dutton.

Jamie may have had his moments of slight revenge—talking to a reporter about the Dutton family sins—but those storylines have all crumbled. In the end, Jamie has done nothing to really merit John’s wrath. If anything, he's made sacrifices to help the Duttons retain control. (Sure, he sold part of his share in the Yellowstone and supports development, but he seems to genuinely believe he's doing these things for the sake of the ranch.) He also literally killed someone.

The reason behind John's wrath may be Beth. Jamie had once taken Beth to an abortion clinic where she became infertile. Perhaps Beth’s grudge has also become John’s. But this is difficult to discern. Again, up until episode 7, it seemed as if John would give Jamie another chance.

But at the moment John learned that Governor Perry would vacate her seat and endorse either John or Jamie, John exploded, claiming that Jamie would be nefarious occupant of the seat. Why? It’s not totally clear.

Fans are confused at John’s sudden vindictiveness. Under a post called “John is so fucking spiteful” one Redditor noted the inconsistencies in John’s treatment of Jamie—how he thwarts Jamie at every turn, despite Jamie’s demonstrable loyalty. “Does [John] not realize Jamie has covered up all the illegal shit they’ve done? Or all the dead bodies from the end of S3? John seems like a smart guy, but what a dumb move. It would have been more wise to let Jamie run and be the puppet master he has always been.”

Another Redditor brought up the natural counter argument: “I don’t see it as spiteful, Jamie has shown time and again he can’t be trusted with power. He also knows his biological father tried to kill his family and he has done nothing with that information.”

Other fans were unhappy at the way in which the episode revealed to Jamie the “twist”—that Governor Perry would endorse John. The governor’s not informing Jamie about the endorsement seemed unrealistic, and the final reveal overly melodramatic.

“I really hate this storyline and where it's headed,” wrote another Redditor. “What's the point? And why do people continue to celebrate John? He's the biggest villain on the show.”

But if John is the villain, he’s a monster that may have just created a new monster. Some fans now foresee Jamie turning into the show’s next villain.

That move would make a lot of sense given Jamie’s past treatment. And now that he knows the truth about Riggins and Garrett, John’s final betrayal may be enough to turn Jamie, finally, into Brutus.
