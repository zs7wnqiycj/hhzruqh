WHEN SOMEONE TALKS to you about Stephen King's It, there's one thing that comes to mind, whether they're first thinking of the original 1986 novel, the 1990 miniseries, or the film series that began with a pair of films in 2017 and 2019: the demented evil clown known as Pennywise. But as It: Welcome to Derry, HBO's prequel series to those two films, has done an exceptional job in making clear, Pennywise the Dancing Clown isn't just a dancing clown. Pennywise (or the evil entity that sometimes materializes as Pennywise, also known as the Galloo) takes many forms—as long as they'll scare you.

In Welcome To Derry, that's materialized in the form of an evil, winged baby. It's materialized as Lily's deceased father in sliced-up, pickle jar-captured form. It's materialized to poor Margie as her eyes popping out of her face like cones. Welcome to Derry has made clear that Pennywise can take any form, and the show's fifth episode, "Neibolt Street" does a particularly good job of hammering that home. And then just as soon as we really get that point made, the show plays the hits—and we finally see Pennywise take that famous clown form.

Not only is it a well-timed slow reveal, but it puts the perfect emphasis on the first appearance of an iconic character that we've all known is coming, and that we have all been waiting for.
