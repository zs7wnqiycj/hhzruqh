What to Consider

Materials

When it comes to choosing the best beanies for men, the material is key to how it feels, performs, and looks. Here’s a breakdown of the most common materials to keep an eye out for.

Wool

Wool is a classic choice for beanies because it’s naturally warm and insulating. It has great moisture-wicking properties, meaning it can pull sweat away from your skin and still keep you warm, even if it gets a little wet. Wool is also durable and has a bit of stretch, so it keeps its shape well. The downside? Some types of wool can feel itchy, though many beanies now use softer, fine wool like Merino to avoid that issue.

Cashmere

Cashmere beanies are all about luxury. It’s incredibly soft, lightweight, and warm, making it one of the most comfortable materials you can wear. Cashmere comes from the undercoat of goats, and its fibers are finer than regular wool, giving it an ultra-soft, plush feel. While it’s pricier than other materials, cashmere beanies are breathable and provide excellent warmth without the bulk. They’re great for both function and style, but you might want to save them for everyday wear rather than rugged outdoor activities since they’re more delicate.

Cotton

Cotton beanies are lightweight and breathable, making them a good choice for transitional weather or if you’re in a mild climate. Cotton is soft and comfortable, and it doesn’t have the same itch factor that wool might. However, it’s not as warm as wool or cashmere, and it lacks moisture-wicking abilities. Cotton beanies are perfect for casual wear or if you want a thinner option for layering, but they won’t keep you as warm in freezing temperatures.

Synthetic Blends (Acrylic, Polyester, Nylon)

Many beanies, especially performance-oriented ones, are made with synthetic materials like acrylic, polyester, or nylon. These materials are affordable, durable, and often mimic the warmth of wool without the itch. They’re usually machine-washable and hold their shape well, making them low-maintenance and long-lasting

Style

Beanies come in a variety of styles, each offering its own vibe and function.

Watch Cap Beanie

This is a classic style, usually made of thicker wool or knit material, with a fold-up cuff that adds extra warmth around the ears. The watch cap gives off a rugged, utilitarian look and pairs well with workwear, peacoats, or military-inspired outerwear.

Fisherman Beanie

This style is shorter, often sitting higher on the head and leaving the ears exposed. It has a roll-up brim and is popular in streetwear and casual styles. Fisherman beanies give off a modern, minimalist vibe and are great for layering with hoodies, casual jackets, and slim-cut coats.

Slouchy Beanie

Known for its relaxed fit, the slouchy beanie has extra fabric that creates a looser, draped look toward the back of the head. It’s ideal for a laid-back, effortless style and works well with oversized outerwear or streetwear-inspired looks.

Cuffed Beanie

A traditional style with a fold-over cuff, similar to a watch cap but often softer and with a more casual fit. Cuffed beanies are versatile and can be worn high on the head for a relaxed look or pulled down to cover more of your forehead and ears for warmth. This style works with almost any outfit, from casual jackets to tailored wool coats.

Ribbed Knit Beanie

Featuring a textured, vertical ribbed knit, this beanie style adds a bit of dimension to your look. It’s usually fitted but can vary between structured and slouchy. Ribbed knit beanies are stylish yet functional, and they pair well with smart casual outfits like tailored coats or wool jackets.

Color

Neutral shades like black, gray, navy, and brown are always solid choices for a beanie. These colors are incredibly versatile and pair easily with pretty much any outfit, making them a go-to for everyday wear. If you’re looking to add some personality to your outfit, bright colors like red, mustard, or forest green are a fun way to mix it up. These colors can make your beanie the focal point of your look and add a pop of color to the often dark and muted shades of fall and winter. Pastel-colored beanies (like soft pinks, baby blues, or light lavenders), are for those who want a more subtle pop of color. These shades feel fresh and modern, giving your winter outfit a lighter, brighter feel in the colder months.

How to Wear Beanies for Men

Wearing beanies the right way will really round out your outfits. To do so, you need to first find a beanie that is well-fitting, and one that suits your style—from classic to slouchy to cuffed. You should also harmonize the color of your beanie with the rest of your outfit for a cohesive look (or use a beanie as a pop of color). Additionally, play with different angles and positions of the beanies—straight on your head, slightly tilted to the side, or pulled back.

And leverage your hair too: If you have longer hair, consider letting some strands peek out from under the beanie for a relaxed and casual vibe; if you have short hair, the beanie can sit lower on your forehead.

Why Is Everyone Wearing Carhartt Beanies?

Carhartt's beanies have gained immense popularity for several reasons. One, they’re rugged and durable, as expected of the workwear brand. Secondly, they’re warm, thanks to the insulating properties in the acrylic blends. Thirdly, they’re versatile and can easily transition from daily travels to hiking to casual outings. The workwear, utilitarian aesthetic with the iconic logo is also a nice touch. But perhaps most importantly, the fact that a Carhartt beanie only costs about $20 and yet is arguably the best beanie of all time is what really makes buyers love it.

Is It Healthy to Wear a Beanie All the Time?

Having a beanie over your head constantly might have some implications for your hair and scalp health. Specifically, wearing a beanie all the time can limit air circulation to your scalp which could potentially make dandruff worse or prevent hair growth. Friction from the beanie against your hair may lead to breakage and damage as well. And if the beanie is not clean or if you sweat while wearing it, it could cause acne on the forehead. Hence, you should always take a break from wearing beanies on a beanie-wearing day to let your scalp breathe, not to mention clean your beanies appropriately.
