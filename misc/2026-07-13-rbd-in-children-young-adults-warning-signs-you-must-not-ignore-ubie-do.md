Can REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) occur in younger adults or children?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) can occur in children, though it is much less common than in older adults. In pediatric cases, RBD is often linked to underlying conditions such as narcolepsy, brainstem tumors, neurodevelopmental disorders (like autism), or the use of certain medications, including antidepressants. Children with RBD may act out vivid dreams by talking, shouting, kicking, or thrashing during sleep, which can lead to injury.

Because RBD in children can signal an underlying neurological or sleep-related condition, early evaluation is important. What is RBD?

During REM sleep, most people experience atonia, which means their muscles are temporarily paralyzed. This prevents them from acting out their dreams. In RBD, this paralysis doesn't happen, so people might move or talk in their sleep.

Occurrence in Children:

Though less common, children can have RBD. It is important for parents to observe any unusual sleep behaviors in their kids, such as talking, yelling, or moving during sleep.
