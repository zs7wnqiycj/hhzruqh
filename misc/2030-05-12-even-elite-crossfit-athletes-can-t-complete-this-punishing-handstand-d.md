# Even Elite CrossFit Athletes Can’t Complete This Punishing Handstand/Deadlift Workout

We're almost done with the CrossFit Open, a five-week competition where athletes compete for the chance to duke it out at the CrossFit Games this August. Each Thursday, Dave Castro, the director of the Open, announces a new workout. While last week's workout 18.3 featured CrossFitters around the world rising to the greatness of the Open, with some even hitting a ring muscle-up for the first time, this week proved to be a set-back for many, with a new handstand push-up standard proving frustrating for even some of CrossFit's elite athletes.

To save you some anguish, we've enlisted Brent Fikowski, a CrossFit Games silver medalist in 2017, to provide some guidance on how to do 18.4 like a champ (or without falling all over yourself — either one).

How to do it

1) Break up the sets.

While it might seem heretical to suggest breaking up the sets during a CrossFit Open workout, Fikowski recommends resting often during 18.4: "If that deadlift feels heavy and the handstand push-ups feel heavy, then don't be afraid to break it up early and often." According to him, the first part of the workout — the 21-15-9 deadlift/handstand pushup couplet known as Diane in the CrossFit world — should take about twice as long as it normally does, "unless your Diane time is creeping around the nine-minute mark, and then just go for it."

[Deadlift Workout](https://therapy.pepgen.cc/article/ot-ii-mice-e3062828/)

If you're more experienced and you want to maintain a competitive pace, Fikowski suggests timing the length of your rest based on breaths, not the clock. "Maybe do three deadlifts, drop the bar, and take one deep breath," he says. "Then pick up the bar again. The deeper you get into the workout, the longer those breaths will probably have to be."

2. Practice your handstand form ahead of time.

This year featured a brand-new handstand push-up standard: an athlete's height plus half the length of his forearm. As Fikowski points out, shorter athletes with long forearms will have a tougher time with this one, but everyone can meet the standard with proper form. "My hands were very close to the wall," says Fikowski. "They were quite close together. I was reaching with my elbows, I was keeping my core tight, and I was squeezing my butt and pulling my toes down."

Fikowski recommends focusing on perfecting your form over meeting the rep requirement. "Every time you're strict pressing or you're inverted, reach that standard, even if it means that you don't do as many reps," he says.

3. If you make it to the handstand walks or bear crawl, dig deep.

If you're able to finish Diane and 21 deadlifts at 315 pounds, then you're probably an elite CrossFitter. Regardless, when you kick up to start that 50-foot handstand walk, be careful: "It's definitely going to be some of the hardest handstand walking you've ever done," says Fikowski. "I know it was for me."

[Even Elite](https://therapy.pepgen.cc/compare/hexarelin-vs-ghrp-2-ghrp-6-and-ipamorelin-potency-and-selectivity-2fc077560b51/)

However, if you're doing the workout scaled, there's a good chance you'll make it to the bear crawl. In that case, "your breathing, your core, and your arms are going to be pretty fatigued," says Fikowski. But if you're feeling up to it, "get moving and get back to the deadlifts, where you can rest in between sets."
