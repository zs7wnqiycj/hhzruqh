First identified in 2015, MOTS-C is unique because it is encoded inside mitochondrial DNA rather than in genes in the nucleus like most proteins. It is thought to work by mimicking many of the biological effects of exercise.

Exercise opens up multiple metabolic pathways, and so does MOTS-C. This is primarily through activation of AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase). AMPK is important in regulating the balance of energy in cells. When switched on, AMPK has a number of downstream effects. It stimulates glucose uptake, increases fat metabolism, and boosts mitochondrial function. People online have reported noticeable differences in muscular endurance and overall reduced fatigue after several weeks on the MOTS-C peptide. One combined animal and human study found physical improvements in gait and grip strength.

So what does this have to do with obesity? Well, mitochondrial dysfunction is common among people with obesity. Plus, the disease can come with lethargy and make a person prone to prediabetes. There is some animal and limited human data that suggests MOTS-C could stop insulin resistance and diet-induced obesity. Scientists are currently recruiting people for a 12-week clinical trial to test the effectiveness of MOTS-C in adults with obesity and prediabetes.

The FDA is interested in MOTS-C’s potential in treating obesity as well as bone health. It’s one of seven peptides under review when an advisory committee convenes on July 23 and July 24, 2026. If the FDA decides to move forward with the recommendations, MOTS-C could be moved to the 503A Bulks List, which features ingredients that the FDA allows pharmacists and doctors to use for compounded medications.
