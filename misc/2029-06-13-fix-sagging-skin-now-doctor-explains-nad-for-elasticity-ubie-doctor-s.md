How Your Doctor Explains NAD+ for Improving Skin Elasticity

Aging skin often becomes thinner, drier and less elastic over time. In recent years, NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) has emerged as a molecule of interest in the anti-aging and dermatology fields. As your doctor might explain, NAD+ plays a fundamental role in cellular energy, DNA repair and collagen production—all key factors for maintaining skin elasticity.

What Is NAD+?

NAD+ is a coenzyme found in every living cell.

It's essential for redox reactions, where it transfers electrons and helps convert nutrients into cellular energy (ATP).

Activates sirtuins, proteins involved in DNA repair and metabolic regulation.

Supports PARP enzymes, which help fix DNA damage.

Modulates inflammatory responses and mitochondrial health.

By keeping cells energized and repairing damage, NAD+ contributes to healthier, more resilient skin.

The Link Between NAD+ and Skin Elasticity

Skin elasticity depends largely on two structural proteins: collagen and elastin. As we age, production of these proteins slows, leading to sagging, fine lines and wrinkles. NAD+ impacts skin elasticity through several mechanisms:

Collagen Synthesis

Sirtuins (especially SIRT1) require NAD+ to function.

Activated SIRT1 promotes collagen gene expression and inhibits enzymes (MMPs) that break down collagen.

DNA Repair and Cell Renewal

NAD+ fuels PARP enzymes that repair DNA damage in skin cells caused by UV radiation and oxidative stress.

Better DNA repair supports healthier, faster-renewing skin cells.

Mitochondrial Function

Healthy mitochondria supply energy for skin cell turnover and barrier maintenance.

NAD+ preserves mitochondrial integrity, reducing signs of cellular aging.

Inflammation Modulation

Chronic, low-grade inflammation accelerates skin aging.

NAD+ regulates inflammatory pathways, helping to maintain balanced immune responses in skin.

What the Research Says

Clinical data on NAD+ supplements and skin elasticity is still emerging. While more large-scale human trials are needed, preliminary findings include:

Cell culture studies show that increasing NAD+ levels enhances collagen production in fibroblasts (the cells responsible for making collagen).

Animal models demonstrate that NAD+ precursor supplements can reduce skin thinning and improve wound healing.

Pilot human trials using nicotinamide riboside or nicotinamide mononucleotide report improvements in cellular markers of aging, though direct measurements of skin elasticity remain limited.

Overall, research supports the biological plausibility of NAD+ enhancing skin health, but conclusive proof of its effects on skin elasticity in humans is still under investigation.
