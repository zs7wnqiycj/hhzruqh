What Is Wegovy?

Wegovy is a once-weekly injectable medication that mimics a hormone called GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). It controls appetite and helps you feel full, leading to weight loss. Approved by the FDA in 2021 for chronic weight management, it's generally safe, but like all drugs, it can have side effects.

Reported Mood Changes

Clinical trials and real-world use have documented:

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (up to 60% of patients)

Headache and fatigue

Rare reports of mood swings and depression

Most mood-related side effects are mild, but some people experience significant sadness or even suicidal thoughts.

Why Does Wegovy Cause Depression?

There isn't a single proven answer yet, but researchers propose several hypotheses:

Direct Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects

GLP-1 receptors exist in brain regions that regulate mood and reward.

Altered signaling might reduce dopamine release, affecting pleasure and motivation.

Nutritional Deficiencies

Gastrointestinal side effects can lead to reduced food intake.

Long-term poor nutrition (e.g., low B12, iron) is linked to depressive symptoms.

Rapid Weight Loss Stress

Losing large amounts of weight quickly can be emotionally taxing.

Body image shifts, loose skin concerns, or changes in social dynamics may trigger anxiety or low mood.

Indirect Hormonal Fluctuations

Changes in insulin sensitivity and thyroid function can influence energy levels and mood stability.

Individual Vulnerability

A personal or family history of depression may increase risk.

Stressful life events or concurrent medications (e.g., corticosteroids) could compound effects.

How Common Is Depression on Wegovy?

In phase 3 trials, mood-related adverse events occurred in fewer than 1% of participants.

Post-marketing surveillance suggests a small but notable number of patients report depressive symptoms.

Real-world data is still emerging; individual experiences vary.
