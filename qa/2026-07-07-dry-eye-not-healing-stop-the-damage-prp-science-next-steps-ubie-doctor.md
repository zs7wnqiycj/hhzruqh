Persistent dry eye is often caused by underlying conditions such as meibomian gland dysfunction, tear gland insufficiency, chronic inflammation, environmental or lifestyle factors, and certain medications. Because of these root causes, over-the-counter drops alone rarely provide lasting relief.

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) therapy uses growth factors from your own blood to promote healing, reduce inflammation, and stabilize the tear film. Effective treatment typically follows a stepwise plan, which may include comprehensive eye exams, artificial tears, omega-3 supplements, prescription anti-inflammatories, IPL therapy, punctal plugs, or scleral lenses.

Incomplete Diagnosis• Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD): Blocked oil glands on your eyelids can cause rapid tear evaporation.• Aqueous‐Deficient Dry Eye: Your tear glands may not produce enough watery tears.• Inflammation: Chronic inflammation on the ocular surface prevents healing.

Understanding PRP for Dry Eye Syndrome

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is emerging as a promising therapy for stubborn dry eye. Here's what you need to know:

What Is PRP?

PRP is a concentration of your own blood platelets suspended in a small volume of plasma. Platelets release growth factors and proteins that:

Promote tissue repair

Reduce inflammation

How PRP Works on the Ocular Surface

Blood Draw and Centrifugation• A small amount of your blood is spun in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets.

Preparation of Eye Drops• The platelet-rich portion is diluted into a sterile eye-drop solution.

Application• You apply the drops several times a day, delivering healing factors directly to your ocular surface.

Benefits for Dry Eye

Enhanced Healing: Growth factors speed up repair of damaged corneal and conjunctival cells.

Anti-Inflammatory Action: PRP can lower inflammatory markers that perpetuate dry eye.

Improved Tear Film Stability: Healthier ocular surface helps maintain a stronger, longer-lasting tear film.

Safety and Efficacy

Autologous Nature: Using your own blood minimizes risk of allergic reaction or transmission of infection.

Clinical Studies: Early trials and pilot studies show improved comfort, tear film quality, and corneal staining scores in patients receiving PRP for dry eye syndrome.

Professional Guidelines: Many ophthalmologists and optometrists now consider PRP a valid adjunct when standard therapies fail.

Medically Approved Next Steps

Even with PRP promising results, a comprehensive approach offers the best chance for relief. Discuss these steps with your eye care provider:

1. Comprehensive Eye Examination

Tear Film Analysis: Measure tear production (Schirmer's test) and break-up time.

Ocular Surface Staining: Detect areas of corneal damage.

Meibography: Image your meibomian glands to assess blockage or atrophy.

2. First‐Line Treatments

Artificial Tears: Start with preservative-free options, 4–6 times daily.

Warm Compresses & Lid Hygiene: Unclog meibomian glands by gently heating and massaging your eyelids.

Omega-3 Supplements: High-quality fish oil (EPA/DHA) supports healthy tear production.

3. Prescription Therapies

Topical Cyclosporine or Lifitegrast: Reduce ocular surface inflammation.

Short-Term Steroid Drops: Control acute flare-ups (used under supervision).

Integrating PRP into Your Care Plan

If you've tried standard therapies without lasting benefit, PRP can be added to your regimen:

Consult Your Specialist: Ask if your clinic offers PRP eye drops or whether you should be referred to a tertiary care center.

Treatment Schedule: Typical protocols involve PRP drops 4–6 times daily for 4–8 weeks.

Monitoring Progress: Regular follow-ups to assess symptom relief, ocular surface health, and adjust treatment as needed.

Combining PRP with lid hygiene, nutritional support, and anti-inflammatory medications often yields the best outcomes.

When to Seek Immediate Help

While most dry eye symptoms are not emergencies, contact your doctor if you experience:

Sudden vision changes

Severe eye pain

Redness that spreads or worsens rapidly

Sensitivity to light

These could signal a more serious condition that needs prompt medical attention.

Final Thoughts

Non-healing dry eye often has multiple contributing factors. A stepwise, medically approved approach—from lifestyle adjustments and standard therapies to advanced options like PRP for dry eye syndrome—gives you the best chance at relief. Speak to your eye care professional about including PRP in your treatment plan, and always discuss any life-threatening or serious symptoms with a doctor right away.
