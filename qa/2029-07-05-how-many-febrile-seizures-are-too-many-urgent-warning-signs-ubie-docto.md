Frequency: Many children have only one febrile seizure, which often does not lead to epilepsy or other problems. Recurrent seizures: About one-third of children who have one febrile seizure will have another, but multiple seizures (more than three in a year) are less common and may require further assessment. When to seek advice: If your child has frequent febrile seizures, prolonged seizures (lasting more than 15 minutes), or seizures involving only one part of the body, it's important to consult a pediatrician or neurologist.
