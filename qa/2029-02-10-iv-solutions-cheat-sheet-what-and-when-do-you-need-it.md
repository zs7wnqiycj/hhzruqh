What is IV therapy?

IV therapy is often associated with being in the hospital, but it is a delivery method for fluids into the body even if you aren’t dealing with an illness or recovering from an injury. It can be effective in improving general wellness and addressing minor ongoing health concerns. IV therapy can be a valuable tool for a wide variety of health conditions, not only those that would require a hospital visit.

IV solutions are packaged in sealed bags that are connected to long, thin tubes. At the end of the tube is a needle, which is inserted into a vein. The bag is suspended above a patient’s head to allow the fluids to flow through the tube into the bloodstream. Other ingredients, such as vitamins, medications, or other treatments can be injected into the bag to infuse them with the IV solution.

Understanding the tonicity of IV solutions

The volume behavior of cells in a solution (such as IV fluids) is called ‘tonicity’. Each cell has two parts – intracellular, referring to the inside of the cell, and extracellular, referring to the area outside the cell. Both parts contain fluids, which may be traded back and forth to reach their ideal state in a process known as osmosis.

The ideal state of tonicity for cells in a solution is the isotonic state, which means the intracellular and extracellular parts have the same concentration of fluids and solutes which can flow into and out of the cell at the same time.

There are three ways to classify IV fluids:

Isotonic IV solutions have the same concentration of solutes (electrolytes) as blood plasma. These solutions are frequently used to increase fluid volume to treat blood loss, dehydration, and during surgery.

Examples of common isotonic IV solutions include:

5% Dextrose in 0.225% saline (D5W1/4NS)

5% Dextrose in water (D5W). This may also be used as a hypotonic solution in certain scenarios.

0.9% Saline

Hypotonic IV solutions have less solutes than blood plasma. This means cells need to be rehydrated and some swelling may result. These solutions are used to address issues like diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or hyperosmolar hyperglycemia.

Examples of common hypotonic IV solutions include:

0.225% Saline (1/4 NS)

0.33% Saline (1/3 NS)

0.45% Saline (1/2 NS)

How do you know which IV solution you need?

There are three different types of dehydration which are broken down based on the electrolyte to fluid ratio. The most abundant electrolyte in the body is sodium (salt).

[IV Solutions](https://glp-insights.xianfengchem.com/blog/6338807335.html)

Isotonic dehydration: This type of dehydration happens when you lose fluids and electrolytes in equal amounts. Diarrhea is the most common cause of isotonic dehydration.

Hypotonic dehydration: This means your body has lost more electrolytes than fluids. Certain people lose higher amounts of electrolytes when they sweat, which can lead to hypotonic dehydration.

Hypertonic dehydration: Also called hypernatremic dehydration, this means your body has lost more fluids than electrolytes. This type of dehydration typically requires medical attention. Children have a higher risk of developing hypertonic dehydration.

Typically, most common types of dehydration are treated with isotonic fluids. Only a licensed and trained medical provider can properly assess your health and determine the appropriate IV fluids for you.

Why Get IV treatment?

IV therapy is a simple way to deliver vital nutrients directly to the bloodstream. IV solutions contain various vitamins, minerals, and electrolytes that help replenish your body and offer all types of support. When you take vitamins and other supplements by mouth, they have to pass through your digestive tract. This can prevent your body from fully absorbing the beneficial properties of the supplements you take. When you utilize IV therapy to receive nutrients, the IV solutions go directly to your bloodstream, bypassing the digestive tract altogether.
