# This Is How Many Steps It Takes to Change Your Health. It’s Probably Fewer Than You Think.

IN THE LAST 10 years, walking has gotten a serious rebrand. What was once considered a mundane part of day-to-day life is now hailed as a gospel for good health. You’ve likely heard that 10,000 steps a day is the key to better well-being, but here’s the thing: that’s not necessarily true…or even backed by science.

Now don’t get us wrong, we absolutely endorse the concept of walking more, but the constant whiplash of “you need this many steps for heart health!” and “no wait, this many steps for longevity!” is overwhelming. People who do walk 10,000 steps a day are typically healthier than those who don’t walk much at all, but you can actually get some big-deal benefits with a much lower step count.

In an effort to clear the air once and for all, we summed up where the research is now. Here’s the long and the short of it.

How Many Steps Do You Really Need Per Day?

[Fewer Than You](https://tendonforge.dns0663.com/blog/8896378737.html)

WHILE WE'D LOVE to give you a hard-and-fast number on exactly how many steps you need a day, the answer is that it really depends. Generally speaking, though, a lot comes down to age, says Amanda Paluch, PhD, an associate professor at the University of Massachusetts Amherst and leader of the Steps for Health Collaborative. “For adults 60 and above, 6,000 to 8,000 steps per day is sufficient for significant health benefits such as reduced all-cause mortality and improved cardiovascular health, but for younger adults less than 60, the sweet spot is more in the 8,000 to 10,000 steps per day range,” she says.

To get more into the nitty gritty, here’s what we know so far about how many steps it takes to give a boost to three major pillars of overall vitality: brain, bone, and heart health.

Quick disclaimer: In many cases, studies are looking for associations, not sending people out with various “walking prescriptions” to see how many steps did what. So although walking is great no matter how many steps you do, none of these numbers are magic…but it’s worth aiming for at least the minimum.

Brain Health: Starting at 5,000 Steps

When we talk about improved brain health, we’re referring to a variety of outcomes, from incidence of depression to risk for cognitive decline or even Alzheimer’s disease, but here’s what we know about the role walking can play in keeping your mind sharp.

A 2024 meta-analysis of over 96,000 adults found that higher daily step counts are linked to fewer depressive symptoms and a lower risk of developing depression. More notably, the study found that walking as few as 7,000 steps a day was associated with roughly a 31 percent reduction in depression risk, with benefits starting around 5,000 steps.

When it comes to preventing cognitive decline, a recent study published in Nature Medicine study found that in cognitively healthy older adults with elevated amyloid (an abnormal type of protein that can build up in the brain and lead to memory loss and slower processing speed), 5,000 to 7,500 steps a day was linked to slower accumulation of tau protein in the brain. This is especially important because when clumped together, elevated tau protein is a hallmark of cognitive decline, neuronal dysfunction, and neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s, says Rachelle Reed, PhD, ACSM-EP, an exercise physiologist in Athens, Georgia.

Final tally: For sharper brain health, aim for 5,000 to 7,500 steps per day.

Bone Health: Starting at 6,000 Steps

[Fewer Than You Think](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/blog/0627458970.html)

ABOUT 54 MILLION AMERICANS currently live with osteoporosis (advanced bone density loss) or osteopenia (moderate bone density loss), and the risk rises with age. Given such, applying controlled stresses to your bones is crucial for maintaining bone density and promoting bone growth, says Amanda Kahn, MD, a board-certified internist and longevity expert. Two to three days of strength training a week is the bread and butter for stronger bones, but there’s also evidence that walking can contribute some benefits.

A Preventive Medicine study of over 1,100 older women in China found that daily step count is an independent protective factor for bone health, because walking at any pace for a cumulative duration improved bone quality. In fact, those who walked 10,000 or more steps per day had significantly healthier bones compared to those walking fewer than 6,000 steps. As a result, the findings suggest that increasing overall steps, rather than focusing on speed, may help maintain bone health and reduce osteoporosis risk in older folks.

And yes, although the study focused on women, Paluch says the same findings likely apply to men. “My research team has examined the role gender plays in nearly every health outcome we’ve looked at, and we have consistently found that the results are similar whether you're male or female.” Incorporating resistance training a few times each week also supports bone health for both men and women.

Final tally: For stronger bones, aim for 6,000 to 10,000 steps per day (along with weekly resistance training).

Heart Health: Starting at 6,000 Steps

HEART DISEASE IS the leading cause of death for all adults in the United States, but making smart daily choices can seriously decrease your risk. We know sitting for long periods of time has never been hailed as healthy, so along with a balanced diet and solid sleep schedule, walking is a great way to keep your ticker in top shape.

A study published in JAMA followed 3,101 adults who wore activity trackers to measure their daily steps. Researchers then compared step counts with mortality rates and found that people who walked at least 8,000 steps on one or two days per week had a lower risk of dying from any cause or heart disease. More specifically, walking 8,000 steps on just one or two days a week was linked to a 15 percent lower risk of death over the next 10 years, while doing so three to seven days a week lowered the same risk by about 16.5 percent.

Another meta-analysis of 20,152 people spanning 6.2 years found that adults older than 60 who took 6,000 to 9,000 steps per day had a 40 to 50 percent lower risk of cardiovascular disease compared to those taking just 2,000 daily steps.

Final tally: For optimal heart health, aim for 6,000 to 9,000 steps per day.

How To Increase Your Daily Steps

Going from zero to 6,000 steps a day can be a shock to the system, so if your goal is to increase daily activity, Reed recommends starting small and keeping it simple. “A good goal is to add about 3,000 extra steps a day, at least a few days per week, which equates roughly 30 minutes of walking,” she says.

It’s often easier to break up your overall step count throughout the day, but if you prefer to knock it all out at once, Paluch says to go for it. “The major takeaway is to get your steps in any way that works for you and your lifestyle, because the strongest relationship to improved health is your total volume of steps, not necessarily when or how you do them,” she says. However, if you’re looking to up the intensity, Dr. Kahn recommends adding a weighted vest for added resistance.
