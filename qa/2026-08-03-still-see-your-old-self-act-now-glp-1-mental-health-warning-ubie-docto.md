# Still See Your Old Self? Act Now: GLP-1 Mental Health Warning | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

Doctor's Note: Losing Weight But Still Seeing Your Old Self? Mental Health on GLP-1s

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Rapid weight loss on GLP-1 medications like Mounjaro can outpace your brain's ability to adjust its body image, leaving you feeling stuck in your old body despite visible changes in the mirror. Common challenges include loose skin, emotional attachments to your former weight, mood swings, anxiety, and an increased risk of body dysmorphia.

Key mental health strategies to support your GLP-1 journey:

Work with a therapist experienced in weight-loss transitions

Join GLP-1 or body image support groups

Practice mindfulness and body neutrality techniques

Consult your healthcare team for personalized care

Because these symptoms can overlap with other treatable conditions—like thyroid issues, depression, or medication side effects—it's important to understand exactly what's driving how you feel. Explanation

Many people turning to GLP-1 medications like Mounjaro (tirzepatide) experience striking weight loss. Yet, some still feel stuck in their "old body" mentally— a mismatch between appearance and self-image. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. Below is an overview of what's happening and how to navigate it.

Understanding GLP-1 Medications and Weight Loss

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) agonists help control blood sugar and reduce appetite. Mounjaro, originally approved for type 2 diabetes, now has widespread off-label use for weight management.

Key effects of GLP-1s:

Slowed gastric emptying → feeling full longer

Reduced hunger signals in the brain

Improved insulin sensitivity

Clinical studies show average loss of 15–20% body weight over 6–12 months.¹ But rapid physical change can outpace mental adjustment.

Why You May "Still See" Your Old Self

1. Body Image Adaptation Lag

Even with clear evidence in the mirror, your brain may continue to process the body you once had. This delay is natural— your self-image is a deep-seated mental model.

2. Loose Skin & Physical Reminders

Significant weight loss often comes with loose or sagging skin. These reminders can trigger dissatisfaction:

Chest, abdomen, arms, thighs

Stretch marks or scars

3. Emotional Attachments

Weight can carry emotional stories (comfort, security, identity). Losing it may feel like losing a part of yourself.

4. Body Dysmorphia Risk

If negative thoughts about your appearance become intrusive— focusing on "flaws" despite overall improvement— you may be experiencing body dysmorphia.²

What Is Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD)?

Body dysmorphic disorder is a mental health condition where a person obsesses over perceived defects in appearance. Even minor or imagined flaws can cause significant distress.

Mental Health Considerations on Mounjaro

While Mounjaro can be life-changing in terms of weight and metabolic health, it isn't a cure-all for mental well-being. Some patients report:

Mood swings: Appetite suppression and rapid change can affect mood regulation.

Anxiety or depression: Underlying or new symptoms may surface as you adapt to a different self-image.

Self-esteem shifts: Initial euphoria may give way to new insecurities.

Action Steps

Track your mood in a journal or app.

Be honest with your prescribing doctor about mental health changes.

Don't stop medication abruptly if you suspect mood effects— discuss options first.

Myths vs. Facts

Myth: "If I lose weight quickly, my mental health will automatically improve."Fact: Physical and mental health progress often move at different speeds. Patience and support are key.

Myth: "Loose skin means failure."Fact: Loose skin is a normal part of large weight loss. It's not a reflection of your worth or success.

Myth: "Body dysmorphia only affects people who are overweight."Fact: BDD can occur at any size or shape.

Final Thoughts

Weight loss with Mounjaro can open doors to better physical health, but the mind doesn't always catch up immediately. Recognizing the gap between the "new you" and your mental image is the first step toward closing it. With the right strategies—therapy, support groups, self-compassion, and professional guidance—you can build a positive, realistic relationship with your changed body.

Please remember: if you have any life-threatening symptoms or serious mental health concerns, reach out to your doctor or local emergency services right away. For concerns that aren't emergencies but still need attention, consider checking your symptoms through a Medically approved AI Symptom Checker Chat Bot to receive personalized health guidance before your appointment.
