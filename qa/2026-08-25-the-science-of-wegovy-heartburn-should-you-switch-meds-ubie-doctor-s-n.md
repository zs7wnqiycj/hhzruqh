Wegovy (semaglutide) has become a leading prescription for chronic weight management. As a GLP-1 receptor agonist, it offers significant appetite control and sustained weight loss. However, some users report heartburn or acid reflux. Why Does Wegovy Cause Heartburn?

Heartburn results from stomach acid moving upward into the esophagus. Semaglutide can contribute to this in several ways:

Delayed gastric emptyingGLP-1 receptor agonists slow down how quickly food leaves the stomach. Longer gastric retention increases the chance of acid refluxing into the esophagus.

Lower esophageal sphincter (LES) pressureSome studies suggest GLP-1 agonists may slightly reduce LES tone, making it easier for stomach acid to travel upward.

Increased gastric acid secretionWhile semaglutide itself doesn't directly spike acid production, the shift in gastric motility can alter pH balance.

Dietary and behavioral changesWeight-loss efforts often involve eating smaller, more frequent meals or trying new foods. These adjustments can independently trigger heartburn.

How Common Is Heartburn With Wegovy?

Clinical trial data and real-world reports show that gastrointestinal side effects are among the most frequent issues with Wegovy:

Up to 20–25% of users report some form of acid reflux or heartburn

Most episodes are mild to moderate and appear during dose escalation

Severe or persistent cases are less common, affecting fewer than 5% of patients

Risk Factors for Heartburn on Wegovy

Certain factors raise the likelihood of experiencing heartburn:

Preexisting gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)

Obesity and central adiposity

Managing Heartburn While Staying on Wegovy

You don't necessarily need to stop Wegovy if heartburn occurs. Many strategies can reduce reflux symptoms:

Dietary Adjustments

Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large portions

Avoid trigger foods: spicy dishes, citrus, tomatoes, chocolate, caffeine, and mint

Limit fatty or fried foods that delay gastric emptying further

Meal Timing and Positioning

Finish eating at least 2–3 hours before lying down

Elevate the head of your bed by 6–8 inches to keep acid in the stomach overnight

Stay upright for 30–60 minutes post-meal to aid digestion

Over-the-Counter Remedies

Antacids (e.g., calcium carbonate) for quick relief

H₂ blockers (e.g., ranitidine alternatives like famotidine) for moderate severity

Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs, e.g., omeprazole, esomeprazole) if reflux is frequent

Behavioral Changes

Maintain a healthy weight to reduce abdominal pressure

Avoid tight clothing around the waist

When to Talk to Your Doctor About Switching Medications

Persistent or severe heartburn may signal that Wegovy isn't the right choice for you. Discuss switching if you experience:

Daily or near-daily reflux despite lifestyle modifications and medications

Esophageal pain, difficulty swallowing, or unintended weight loss beyond expectations

Complications like esophagitis or Barrett's esophagus diagnosed on endoscopy

Symptoms that interfere with quality of life, sleep, or daily activities

Your doctor will weigh the benefits of continuing Wegovy against the risks. They may recommend:

Trying another GLP-1 receptor agonist (e.g., liraglutide, which may have slightly different gastrointestinal effects)

Switching to a dual agonist like tirzepatide, which some patients tolerate better

Moving to an entirely different weight-loss class (e.g., bupropion/naltrexone, orlistat)

Adding a more potent anti-reflux medication under medical supervision

Alternative Medications for Weight Management

If you and your provider decide that Wegovy isn't suitable, here are some alternatives:

Liraglutide (Saxenda)A daily GLP-1 agonist with similar benefits, but users report varied tolerability.

Tirzepatide (Mounjaro)Dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist showing robust weight-loss results; gastrointestinal side effects differ for each patient.

Bupropion/Naltrexone (Contrave)A combination that targets central appetite pathways, often with fewer GI side effects.

Orlistat (Xenical, Ali)Works by reducing fat absorption. May cause gastrointestinal discomfort, but less heartburn.

Your choice depends on your medical history, comorbid conditions, insurance coverage, and personal preferences.

Monitoring and Follow-Up

Regular check-ins with your healthcare provider are key to safe, effective weight management:

Schedule visits every 4–12 weeks during dose adjustments

Report any new or worsening heartburn, chest pain, or difficulty swallowing promptly

Consider an upper endoscopy if heartburn persists beyond 8–12 weeks despite treatment

Keep a symptom diary tracking food intake, medication timing, and reflux episodes

Balancing Benefits and Side Effects

Wegovy offers substantial benefits for weight loss, improved metabolic health, and reduced cardiovascular risk. Heartburn is often manageable, especially when caught early. Before switching therapies:

Try conservative measures first (diet, positioning, OTC meds)

Work closely with your doctor or a gastrointestinal specialist

Weigh the pros and cons of continuing versus changing medication

Final Thoughts

Understanding why does Wegovy cause heartburn helps you make informed decisions and manage side effects without undue worry. Many people continue Wegovy successfully by combining lifestyle tweaks with targeted reflux treatments. However, if heartburn significantly disrupts your life or poses health risks, exploring alternative weight-loss medications is reasonable.

Remember, nothing in this guide replaces personalized medical advice. Always speak to a healthcare professional about any serious or life-threatening symptoms, and don't hesitate to discuss medication changes.
