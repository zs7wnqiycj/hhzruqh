Why Primary Care Doctors Miss Deep Sinus Polyps: The Need for Endoscopy

Chronic nasal congestion, facial pressure, loss of smell and recurring sinus infections can all point to nasal polyps—benign growths of the lining inside your sinuses or nasal passages. While primary care doctors (PCPs) are often the first medical professionals you see for these complaints, they can miss deep sinus polyps. Understanding why this happens and when you may need an endoscopic evaluation can help you get the right diagnosis and treatment sooner.

Why Primary Care Doctors Miss Diagnosing Deep Sinus Polyps

Primary care doctors are skilled at diagnosing and treating a broad range of conditions, but detecting deep sinus polyps presents unique challenges:

Overlapping Symptoms• Allergies, common colds and chronic sinusitis all share similar complaints: congestion, runny nose, facial pressure, postnasal drip• PCPs may attribute persistent symptoms to allergy flare-ups or recurrent infections rather than polyps

Limited Physical Exam Tools• In a typical office visit, doctors use a penlight and maybe an otoscope• Polyps hidden deep in the middle meatus or ethmoid sinuses often lie beyond the reach of basic instruments

Reliance on Symptom History• Time constraints in primary care visits can emphasize quick history-taking over more detailed nasal exams• Patients may underplay or normalize their symptoms ("I've always had a stuffy nose.")

Lack of Specialized Training• Detecting and grading sinus polyps by touch and sight requires otolaryngology (ENT) training• PCP residency programs cover many topics; nuanced endoscopic technique may receive less focus

Misinterpretation of Imaging• If imaging is ordered, a standard X-ray or non-ENT–focused CT might not be interpreted with polyp suspicion in mind• Radiology reports can flag "mucosal thickening" or "sinus opacification" without explicitly noting polyps

Treatment First, Diagnosis Later• It's common to start nasal steroids or antibiotics before referring to a specialist• Improvement in symptoms can also mask the underlying growths, delaying further evaluation

How Endoscopy Bridges the Gap

Diagnostic nasal endoscopy—using a thin, flexible scope with a camera and light—is the gold standard for visualizing deep sinus structures. Here's why it matters:

• Direct Visualization You see polyps, secretions and structural variations in real time.• Precise Location Endoscopy pinpoints which sinus cavities are involved (e.g., frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid).• Grading Severity Polyps are graded by size (Grade 1–4) and extent, which guides treatment plans.• Biopsy Capability Suspicious lesions can be sampled immediately if there's any uncertainty.

Treatment Options for Nasal Polyps

Once deep sinus polyps are confirmed, treatment typically follows a stepwise approach:

• Medical Management – Intranasal steroids (fluticasone, mometasone) – Short courses of oral steroids for large polyps – Biologic therapies (e.g., dupilumab) for refractory cases

• Surgical Intervention Functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) to remove polyps and restore drainage • Balloon sinuplasty for mild disease in selected sinus openings

• Long-Term Follow-Up Regular nasal endoscopies to monitor regrowth Maintenance intranasal steroids
