# Are You Guilty of These Common Skincare Mistakes?

YOU TAKE CARE of your body: You lift heavy, get your cardio in, eat clean(ish), maybe even track your macros. But are you giving the same level of care to your skin health?

It’s not enough to wash your face, slap on some aftershave, and call it skincare—especially if you’re guilty of certain daily habits that could be doing damage over time. Some seemingly harmless choices can add up to dryness, breakouts, irritation, premature aging, and even an increased risk for skin cancer.

The good news is that you don't need a 10-step routine. A few simple fixes can go a long way toward optimizing the health of your largest organ—your skin.

8 Surprising Habits That Are Hurting Your Skin

1. Skipping Daily SPF

Yeah, yeah, you've heard this one before. But there’s a reason this is at the top of every skincare list: Skipping out on SPF is one of the biggest lifestyle habits that can hurt your skin, and “sunscreen is the ultimate line of defense against harmful sun exposure and UV radiation,” says Michele Green, MD, a cosmetic dermatologist in New York, NY.

Only 12.3 percent of men aged 18 and older always used sunscreen when outside for more than an hour on a sunny day, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The truth is—rain or shine, winter or summer—without that daily protection, you’re leaving your skin vulnerable to hyperpigmentation, accentuated wrinkles, premature aging, and skin cancer. That also goes for all skin tones: People with darker skin do get some extra protection in the form of melanin, but it's still important to wear SPF.

This also applies to more than just your face. “Men often forget to apply sunblock to the top of the ears, which is a common location for skin cancer, particularly in men with short hair,” says Brian Toy, MD, a dermatologist at Providence Mission Hospital in Orange County, CA, and a clinical professor in the School of Medicine at the University of Southern California. “And bald men often forget to apply sunblock to the scalp, another common location for skin cancer.” Make sure you’re using at least SPF 30, as this will protect against 97 percent of UV rays. Anything less and your skin will be vulnerable to sun damage.

2. Not Washing Your Sheets Regularly

If you're battling breakouts and irritation, the issue may not be in your skincare routine but between your sheets. “Men’s skin, naturally oilier due to higher testosterone, produces more sebum, which, along with sweat, transfers to pillowcases and sheets during sleep,” explains Angelo Ayar, MD, a dermatologist specializing in surgical, cosmetic, and medical dermatology in south Florida. That creates a ripe environment for bacteria and allergens like dust mites, which can trigger breakouts, redness, and eczema flare-ups—and sleeping atop unwashed bedding can exacerbate “acne mechanica, a type of acne caused by friction and pressure from prolonged contact with contaminated surfaces,” he adds.

FYI: More than a quarter of Americans only wash their sheets once a month, according to a 2025 survey by Amerisleep; older research has shown single men wait on average 45 days before changing their bedding. To protect your skin, Dr. Ayar advises washing sheets weekly in hot water (at least 60°C or 140°F) and to consider using silk or bamboo pillowcases and sheets, which are less absorbent of oils, wash sheets with fragrance-free detergents, and shower before bed to minimize oil and contaminant transfer.

3. Using Body Soap On Your Face

Body soaps and bar soaps are too harsh for facial skin, which is thinner and more sensitive than body skin. As a result, “body soaps will strip the face of its protective oils, leading to dryness, irritation, and worsening of fine lines and wrinkles,” explains Dr. Toy.

4. Not Using Moisturizer

Skipping moisturizer might seem like a low-maintenance move, but it can backfire. “Many men—especially those with oily or acne-prone skin—avoid moisturizers altogether, thinking they’ll clog pores or make their face shinier,” says William Shipman, MD, PhD, a dermatologist in Pasadena, CA. But “dehydrated skin can overcompensate by producing more oil, leading to breakouts and an uneven texture.”

This is even more important for older men. “As men age, their skin becomes drier due to a decline in oil production. This oil, called sebum, serves as a natural moisturizer and floats on the surface of the skin to prevent evaporation of water,” says Dr. Toy. “A moisturizing cream mimics the action of natural sebum and restores the skin barrier to reduce water loss.” You don’t need anything fancy, says Toy, just look for a lightweight moisturizer with oil-based emollients like ceramides, silicone, and shea butter, which help form a protective barrier, or water-based humectants like glycerin, aloe vera, and hyaluronic acid, which draw in moisture from the air and deeper layers of the skin to lock in hydration, calm inflammation, and protect against signs of aging.

5. Delaying the Post-Gym Shower

Skipping a shower after your workout might save time, but leaving sweat-soaked gym clothes on (especially tight ones) traps moisture against your skin and allows bacteria to thrive in the humid environment. “It’s very important to get out of soiled clothing immediately, otherwise you’re asking for clogged pores, body acne, and fungal infections,” says Dr. Kim.

And keep the water lukewarm. Turning the dial all the way to “scalding” (whether you're in the shower or washing your face at the sink) “can dry out the skin, damage its natural barrier, and lead to redness, dryness, and sensitivity,” he adds. If you prefer the heat, keep it to five minutes max; otherwise, a lower temp is gentler on your skin and still gets the job done.

6. Neglecting Proper Shaving Hygiene

Men’s facial hair grows thick and fast, so it’s important to practice proper shaving technique and hygiene in order to prevent microtears (or abrasions in the skin that may not be visible but provide an opening to irritants and microbes), irritation, hyperpigmentation, and ingrown hairs, says Dr. Ayar. Dr. Green adds that men with coarse or curly hair are more susceptible to razor bumps. "After shaving, coarse or curly hair is more likely to curl and get trapped beneath the skin, resulting in razor bumps and ingrown hairs," she says.

Plan to pick up your razor every one to three days, depending on hair growth (over-shaving can cause microtears and inflammation). “Before you shave, use a gentle facial cleanser to remove dirt and oil, then soften hair with warm water or a damp towel for one to two minutes to reduce friction (or shave immediately after a shower, when your pores are open),” he says. “Apply a lubricating shave gel or cream—no soap—and use a sharp, clean single- or double-blade razor. Rinse the blade after each pass to avoid bacterial buildup and to free the blades of hair. When you’re done, rinse with cool water, pat dry, and apply a soothing, alcohol-free moisturizer with aloe or niacinamide to calm the skin.”

7. Picking at Ingrown Hairs, Pimples, or Rough Patches

Everyone does it. “But men—especially those who shave or have coarse facial hair—are more prone to ingrown hairs and often try to ‘dig them out,” says Dr. Shipman. “This habit almost always causes more harm than good: scarring, hyperpigmentation, and even infections.” And a pimple may start small, but “acne can be non-inflammatory, clogged with dead skin cells, dirt, excess oil, and other impurities, or inflammatory, when bacteria are also trapped in the pores,” says Dr. Green. “Picking at a pimple can spread the pore’s contents into other areas of the skin and result in more acne, skin inflammation, scarring, and even spread the bacterial infection.”

Dr. Green says that individuals with darker skin tones are often more susceptible to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, or dark spots, after picking at their skin. "People with darker skin tones have more melanocytes, which are cells that produce melanin pigment," she explains. "Inflammation from skin picking can trigger these melanocytes to produce excessive pigment, resulting in dark spots in the affected area."

8. Not Grooming Your Beard

Skipping regular washing, never exfoliating, or slathering on thick products that trap gunk can turn your beard into a breeding ground for things that really shouldn’t take up any real estate on your face. “Beards are a magnet for microbes, including yeast, fungi, and bacteria,” says Dr. Toy. “The hair, oil, and moisture from around the mouth provide a hospitable environment for microbes to flourish.” That can lead to skin issues like itching, redness, and dandruff-like flaking, plus acne, folliculitis, and dermatitis.
