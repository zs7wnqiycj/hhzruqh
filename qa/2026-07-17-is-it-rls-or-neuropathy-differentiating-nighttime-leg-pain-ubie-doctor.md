# Is it RLS or Neuropathy? Differentiating Nighttime Leg Pain | Ubie Doctor's Note

Is it RLS or Neuropathy? Differentiating Nighttime Leg Pain

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Restless leg syndrome (RLS) and peripheral neuropathy are two distinct conditions that disrupt sleep and daily comfort, but they differ in key ways:

Restless leg syndrome: Causes an irresistible urge to move the legs, often with crawling or pulling sensations at night. Symptoms improve with movement.

Peripheral neuropathy: Results from nerve damage, leading to numbness, burning, or tingling that typically worsens at rest and does not improve with activity.

While both conditions affect quality of life, they have different causes, diagnostic tests, and treatment paths. Identifying which one you're experiencing is the first step toward effective relief.

Understanding Nighttime Leg Pain

Waking up at night with an uncontrollable urge to move your legs or a tingling, burning sensation can be distressing. Two common culprits are restless leg syndrome (RLS) and peripheral neuropathy. While both can disrupt sleep and quality of life, they have distinct features, causes, and treatments. Here's how to tell them apart and what to do next.

What Is Restless Leg Syndrome?

Restless leg syndrome (also called Willis-Ekbom Disease) is a neurological condition that causes:

An irresistible urge to move the legs, often described as crawling, tingling, itching, or pulling

Symptoms that start or worsen at night or when resting

Temporary relief with movement—walking, stretching, or jiggling the legs

Sleep disturbances that can lead to daytime fatigue, irritability, or difficulty concentrating

What Is Peripheral Neuropathy?

Peripheral neuropathy occurs when peripheral nerves (outside the brain and spinal cord) are damaged. It can affect sensory, motor, or autonomic nerves, leading to:

Numbness, tingling, burning, or electric-shock sensations in the feet and legs—sometimes hands and arms

Symptoms often worse at night or after lying down

Sharp pain that may radiate up the limbs

Muscle weakness, loss of reflexes, or balance problems in advanced cases

Common causes cited by the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke:

Diabetes (the most frequent cause)

Vitamin deficiencies (B12, B6)

Alcohol misuse

Infections (Lyme disease, HIV)

Autoimmune disorders (Guillain-Barré syndrome)

Certain medications (chemotherapy drugs)

How Are They Diagnosed?

Medical History & Physical Exam

Your doctor will ask detailed questions about symptoms, family history, and possible triggers.

A neurological exam can assess reflexes, muscle strength, and sensation.

Blood Tests

Check iron levels, kidney function, blood sugar (for diabetes), vitamin B12.

Specialized Tests

Nerve conduction studies or electromyography (EMG) to identify nerve damage.

Sleep studies (polysomnography) if sleep apnea or periodic limb movement disorder is suspected.

Treatment Options

Restless Leg Syndrome

Lifestyle Changes

Establish a regular sleep schedule

Avoid caffeine, nicotine, and alcohol, especially in the evening

Gentle stretching or leg massages before bed

Medications

Dopamine agonists (e.g., pramipexole, ropinirole)

Alpha-2-delta calcium channel ligands (e.g., gabapentin)

Iron supplements if levels are low

When to Seek Medical Advice

Nighttime leg symptoms can sometimes signal serious underlying issues. Reach out to a doctor if you experience:

Sudden onset of severe pain, weakness, or numbness

Symptoms that spread rapidly or affect your ability to walk

Signs of infection—redness, swelling, fever

Chest pain, shortness of breath, or calf swelling (possible blood clot)

For ongoing symptoms, consulting a neurologist or sleep specialist can help pinpoint whether you have neuropathy, restless leg syndrome, or both.

Practical Self-Care Tips

Keep a symptom diary: note time of day, activity level, foods, and medications.

Stay active: regular, moderate exercise can ease both RLS and neuropathy.

Practice good sleep hygiene: cool, dark bedroom; consistent bedtime routines.

Use supportive footwear and avoid tight socks or bedding that might compress nerves.

Final Thoughts

Differentiating neuropathy from restless leg syndrome is crucial for effective treatment. While RLS is driven by an urge to move and relieved by activity, neuropathy involves nerve damage with burning or tingling sensations that don't always improve with movement.
