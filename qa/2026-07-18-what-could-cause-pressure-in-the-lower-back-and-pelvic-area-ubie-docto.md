What could cause pressure in the lower back and pelvic area?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Pressure in the lower back and pelvic area is often caused by muscle strain, pregnancy-related changes, or pelvic floor dysfunction. Other possible causes include urinary tract infections, endometriosis, fibroids, ovarian cysts, or prostate issues. The exact cause depends on your symptoms, medical history, and lifestyle factors.

Because pelvic and lower back pressure can stem from so many different conditions—some minor, others requiring medical attention—identifying the likely cause early is key to getting the right care. Explanation

Pressure in the lower back and pelvic area can be caused by several factors. One common cause is related to muscle problems. The muscles in the lower back and pelvis can develop tight spots, known as myofascial trigger points, which can lead to pain and pressure.

Another cause can be related to the pelvic floor, which is a group of muscles that support the pelvic organs. Problems with these muscles can contribute to pain in the lumbopelvic region.

Additionally, during pregnancy, many women experience low back and pelvic girdle pain. This happens because of changes in the body, such as weight gain and hormonal changes, which can affect the muscles and joints in the pelvic area.
