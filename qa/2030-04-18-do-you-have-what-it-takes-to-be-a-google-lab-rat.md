# ​Do You Have What It Takes to Be a Google Lab Rat?

Google is searching for 10,000 people to serve as lab rats for part of its Project Baseline, a four-year, comprehensive and unprecedented health study, the company announced.

Led by Verily, Duke University School of Medicine, Stanford Medicine, and Google, the study is seeking volunteers to submit to long-term, high-tech health monitoring, with the hopes of understanding diseases better so researchers can learn new ways to prevent and treat them.

So what does it entail? First, you’d wear an investigational, high-tech watch daily, which will track things like heart rate and activity level. You’d slide a sensor under your mattress to monitor your sleep habits. You’d answer a set of surveys every three months about things like diet, exercise, and wellbeing, and fill out a few questions every so often on their mobile app in between.

Once a year, you’d travel to a Baseline site near you for one to two days of health tests, which will include things like echocardiogram, eye exam, chest x-ray. You’ll give blood and saliva samples, as well as answer a detailed questionnaire. Additionally, you’ll schedule three shorter appointments each year so the researchers can gather more specialized data.

The company promises that confidentiality and your privacy is of utmost importance. There are safeguards in place so that your data won’t be able to be linked to you without your permission. (You’ll have to give them access to your health records in order for them to get a clearer picture of your health.)

The goal of the project is to scrutinize everything from your health habits to your genome to find out what makes certain diseases tick, so scientists can find new ways to diagnose and treat them.

Participants will be selected to create a pool that’s representative of the entire population.
