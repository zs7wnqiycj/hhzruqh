# We Ranked Every Upcoming Marvel Movie Until 2021

You are going to be watching Marvel movies until you die, and they will keep going long afterwards. The fun of investing yourself in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, which finishes its long gestating Phase Three with April’s Avengers: Endgame, is that this is all just one long ongoing story: That these universes are all connected. It’s a forever space opera. It’s a perpetual franchise.

Marvel has not confirmed its entire future schedule, other than a vague calendar with terms like “Untitled Marvel Movie 8” scheduled for July 2022. But there are many rumors, and even some confirmed directors and characters. All of these movies are exciting. But how exciting? Today, we rank all confirmed and semi-confirmed Marvel movies, by how thrilled we are (and you should be) to see them. For what it’s worth, we are including the Sony movies (who own Spider-Man and occasionally borrow MCU stars like Iron Man) and the Fox X-Men movies (which haven’t crossed over with Marvel yet but could someday) as part of the Marvel family, even if they are not technically part of the Marvel Cinematic Universe.

14. Gambit (March 2020)

Be worried about this one. Channing Tatum stars as Gambit, a former thief from Louisiana with the power to manipulate kinetic energy. Cool, right? We guess. But the movie has been a mess from the start, with three different directors attached and then leaving the project, from Rupert Wyatt to Doug Liman to Gore Verbinski. It has also apparently switched from a heist film to a romantic comedy (with the great Lizzy Caplan as Gambit’s foil). But it still hasn’t started filming, and it still doesn’t have a director. At last check, Tatum himself was talking about directing it. We’ll see if this one even happens.

13. X-Men: Dark Phoenix (June 2019)

The X-Men have had a rocky road of it lately, with X-Men: Apocalypse a bit of a groaner (and directed by Bryan Singer, who is surely done with these now) and its biggest moment of the last few years getting made fun of in Deadpool 2. They’ve brought the crew back again to follow Jean Grey’s release of the Phoenix, one of the more powerful characters from the comics. You do have to wonder how much longer they’re going to keep this going, with actors like Jennifer Lawrence, Michael Fassbender and James McVoy clearly ready to move on, and the franchise wobbly in Deadpool’s shadow. Focusing a whole movie on a heretofore minor character seems its own risk. Expect X-Men: Deadpool (or vice versa) as soon as they can get the contracts worked out.

12. Venom 2 (TBD)

The first film was a huge hit, which disguised the fact that it was a total mess, pretty much from the get-go. (This movie is basically a series of plot holes with occasionally CGI explosions.) But Tom Hardy had a certain lunatic energy as Venom, and when a movie makes as much money as this did, there will definitely be another one. Maybe they’ll take a little bit more time getting the details right on this one, or, if that doesn’t happen, they'll just let Hardy go crazy. That’d be fine too.

11. Doctor Strange 2 (2021)

The first film was fun, if not particularly inspired; do you even remember anything about Rachel McAdams’ character? We’d argue Doctor Strange was more fun in Infinity War than was in his own movie. Director Scott Derrickson will return for this one, and we’ll confess, we enjoy Benedict Cumberbatch’s American accent … but this feels a little minor to us.

10. The New Mutants (August 2019)

Here’s where the exit ramp might be for the X-Men Franchise: These “new” mutants, focusing on teenager mutants with incredible powers being held in a facility against their will. If this is the big hit Fox is hoping it is, they won’t need to worry about Lawrence and Fassbender and the crew: They’ll have their own, new franchise to build around. Director Josh Boone knows his teenagers; he directed The Fault in Their Stars, after all. We’re not sure we need a whole other X-Men franchise, but the new blood can’t help but inject some life into the enterprise.

9. Black Widow (2020)

No, it won’t be the “Saturday Night Live” version of the story, but it is going to happen: Scarlett Johannsen reportedly signed a $15 million deal to play the character in a solo film, to be directed by Cate Shortland (Berlin Syndrome, Lore). The most intriguing rumor about Black Widow is that it will be rated R, which would be the first Marvel Cinematic Universe movie to receive the rating.

8. Guardians of the Galaxy 3 (TBD)

After the controversy about writer-director James Gunn took him off the project, the third Galaxy movie got pushed back, but they’re still planning on make it, and maybe even using Gunn’s already-finished script. (Though he won’t be behind the camera.) These characters remain grand company, but you can’t help but wonder if it’ll lose something without the maestro who made it all happen. Either way: We’re still mad at Peter Quill for Infinity War. What was that, man?

7. Shang-Chi (TBD)

Announced just last month, Shang-Chi is the first Marvel movie with an Asian lead, based off a character trained in the martial arts and confronting his own family demons. The movie is being written by Dave Callaham, who also wrote the upcoming Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse sequel as well as writing the Wonder Woman sequel. The time seems right for this, and it’s clearly in the right hands.

6. The Eternals (2020)

The first big tentpole of the MCU’s Phase Four, The Eternals are a gaggle of otherworldly beings who have superhuman, well, everything; think less Hawkeye, more Thanos. This is thought to be a major project, the linchpin of the next phase, but we are perhaps most excited because of the director attached: Chloe Zhao, who made 2018’s incredible The Rider, a movie that would seem as different from a massive enterprise like this one as humanly possible.

5. Captain Marvel (March 2019)

The long-overdue first Marvel movie to focus solely on a female lead —the one thing DC beat them to—Captain Marvel stars Brie Larsen as a former Air Force pilot whose DNA is fused with a Kree warrior in an accident and ends up making her a galactic superhero. You may remember the call for Captain Marvel in the closing credits of Infinity War, connecting the two Avengers films, but the timeline takes place in the ‘90s, which means you can probably expect lots of Snapple and rappers with tall hair.

4. Spider-Man: Far From Home (July 2019)

Spider-Man: Homecoming was the most fun Spider-Man movie in a decade … at least until Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse came about. Tom Holland has revitalized the character in the public consciousness, and the result (helped along by a great villain played by Michael Keaton and an overall giddy, teen-bop vibe) was a blast. The sequel features a school trip to Europe and a bad guy named Mysterio, played by Jake Gyllenhaal. Spider-Man fatigue is natural at this point, but director Jon Watts kept things moving the first time, and is back for this one. We’re not quite tired of Spidey yet.

3. Black Panther 2

The highest-grossing Marvel movie ever—and the third-highest-grossing movie of all time—is obviously going toward the very top of excitement list, and even though it’ll lose its incredible villain Killmonger, Wakanda and its people have an endless number of stories to tell us. Ryan Coogler has signed on to direct the sequel, so we’re already in: Heck, it’s possible the first film ends up winning Best Picture, after all. And you never know: Maybe they can find a place for Michael B. Jordan after all.

2. Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse 2 (2020)

This is surely not what this will be called, but if you’ve seen the fantastic original, you know there is no limit to the number of spinoffs that could come from it. We’ll surely get a Noir Spider-Man, and Spider-Woman, and maybe even a Spider-Ham movie at some point, but this one will surely focus on Miles Morales, the hero of the first film. Eventually they will dilute the greatness of the first film, and it will be a bummer. But right now: We want as many of these movies as they will give us.

1. Avengers: Endgame (April 2019)

This is the big one, the second half of the epic, turning on the cliffhanger of (spoilers? we guess?) of Thanos snapping half the universe out of existence. How do they all come back? Do they all come back? How do they take care of Thanos? And perhaps most fascinating: Which heroes, original Avengers, are being retired out of the franchise. Is this it for Captain America? Iron Man? Nick Fury? More than a decades worth of movies have led specifically to this one. All that come after will be a reaction to it. It’s the biggest one yet, and maybe the biggest one ever.
